Seattle signed a quarterback this week. He is a UFL Most Valuable Player who ran for forty-five touchdowns in college. And he is the fourth quarterback on the depth chart.

Both of those things are true at the same time, twelves, and that’s the whole story.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Seattle is 4-4. Sam Darnold is out Sunday with a quad injury and a cracked rib. Drew Lock is starting with a sore elbow. And the Arizona Cardinals are coming to Lumen Field with a brand-new quarterback of their own. Let’s go through all of it — including the one thing in this story that nobody is explaining properly.

First, exactly what happened, because it was four transactions, not a blockbuster.

On Wednesday, November fourth, the Seattle Seahawks signed quarterback Adrian Martinez and linebacker Jamie Sheriff to the practice squad. To make room, they released linebacker Jimmy Ciarlo and wide receiver Malick Meiga.

That’s the deal. Practice squad.

And twelves — Malick Meiga again.

I told you his story two weeks ago. Born in the Ivory Coast, raised in Montreal. Penn State, then Coastal Carolina. Undrafted. Drafted in the second round of the CFL draft by the Hamilton Tiger-Cats. Training camp with Carolina. Signed to Seattle’s practice squad in September. Released in October. Signed back in October.

And released again on Wednesday.

That’s three times Seattle has cut the same man in ten weeks. And I said at the time that this coaching staff keeps a list and doesn’t forget people. I still believe that. But this is also the actual, unglamorous reality of the bottom of a roster: you are the first spot anybody reaches for when the quarterback room gets thin. I hope somebody calls him again, and I’d bet somebody does.

Now — why did Seattle need a quarterback in the first place? Because the room got thin in a hurry.

Sam Darnold is out with a quad injury suffered in the Chicago loss — and he also has a cracked rib from that same game.

He played the whole Bears game on a broken rib. Seventeen of thirty-four, 188 yards, two touchdowns, and nobody knew.

Macdonald says Darnold is “week to week” and will not go on injured reserve — which, remember, means a minimum of four games. They didn’t do it, so they expect him back sooner than that.

And Drew Lock’s right elbow is the real reason Seattle made this move. Lock was limited in practice Wednesday, and Macdonald said: “He’s able to throw. How much, we’re working through that right now.” That elbow is why Lock was inactive against Kansas City and served as the emergency third quarterback instead of the backup.

Macdonald said earlier in the week the team might add a quarterback. Two days later they added one. That’s not panic — that’s a coach doing exactly what he said he’d do.

Now who Adrian Martinez actually is, because his résumé is wilder than you’d think.

He started forty-seven college games — four seasons at Nebraska and one at Kansas State. Sixty-three point six percent completions, 9,752 passing yards, fifty-one touchdowns, thirty-one interceptions.

And then this: 2,928 rushing yards and forty-five rushing touchdowns.

Forty-five rushing touchdowns in college. Twelves, that’s not a quarterback who scrambles. That’s a quarterback who was the run game.

He went undrafted out of Kansas State in 2023, signed with Detroit, and got waived before the regular season. And then he went to the UFL and the Birmingham Stallions — where he won league MVP and championship game MVP.

The championship detail the fan channels got right: he threw a touchdown to former Seahawks draft pick Gary Jennings Jr., added rushing scores, and Birmingham won the title game 25-0. A shutout in a championship game.

Since then he’s spent time with the Jets and the 49ers, and he appeared in one game for San Francisco last season.

And now here is the part that nobody is explaining, and it’s the actual reason Seattle signed him.

Seattle’s own announcement noted that his time with the 49ers should help him learn Seattle’s offense, because the two systems share similarities.

Twelves, that is the whole signing right there.

Seattle’s offense runs out of the same coaching family as San Francisco’s — the wide-zone, play-action, motion-heavy tree. Same concepts, overlapping terminology, similar protections. Klint Kubiak’s offense and Kyle Shanahan’s offense are cousins.

So when you sign a quarterback in November, you are not signing an arm. You are signing a vocabulary.

On a six-day week, after a Monday night game, there is no time to teach a brand-new man a brand-new language. The only street quarterback who can be useful by Sunday is one who already speaks it — who hears “shift to the right, wide zone, play-action off it” and sees the picture instead of a dictionary.

Adrian Martinez isn’t a dream quarterback. He’s a translator who happens to have been a league MVP. And on this particular week, that’s the smarter signing.

And here’s the mechanic that makes him immediately useful without costing a roster spot.

He’s on the practice squad — which means Seattle can elevate him on game day. Remember the rule: two practice-squad players can be elevated per game, each man up to three times a season, and he reverts automatically at four o’clock Eastern on the first business day after the game, with no waivers.

So if Drew Lock’s elbow is bad on Friday, Seattle can activate Adrian Martinez for Sunday and lose nothing. No 53-man spot. No waiver risk. That flexibility is the entire point of the move.

The depth chart as it stands: Drew Lock starts. Jalen Milroe is the backup. Adrian Martinez is the insurance policy.

Now Drew Lock’s case — and it’s genuinely encouraging.

This season: seventy-three percent completions. Eight point eight yards per attempt. Four touchdowns, zero interceptions. A 127.3 passer rating.

And his only start was against Arizona. Week 2, at State Farm Stadium: nineteen of twenty-six, 235 yards, three touchdowns, zero interceptions. Seattle won 31-7.

All three touchdowns went to Jaxon Smith-Njigba. And the first one came on Lock’s very first throw of the game — a play designed to go somewhere else that turned into a deep shot to the best receiver in football.

And the defensive half of that game was brutal. After Arizona scored its only touchdown, their next six full possessions produced five punts and an interception. Three first downs across all six drives.

That’s the movie. And I understand why everybody wants a sequel.

But here’s the most important thing in this entire video, and it’s a correction: Arizona is not the same team.

Gardner Minshew the Second is their quarterback now, and he earned it.

On Sunday against Dallas, Minshew replaced a benched Jacoby Brissett in the third quarter with Arizona trailing 17-3. He went seventeen of twenty-three for 188 yards and three touchdowns, led five scoring drives, and led the game-winning drive in overtime. Cardinals 33, Cowboys 30.

He erased a fourteen-point deficit on the road and won it in overtime.

Mike LaFleur named him the starter the next day. His words: “We needed a spark, and obviously Gardner gave us one.”

And Jacoby Brissett — the quarterback Seattle beat 31-7 — is now Arizona’s third-string quarterback. He went 3-5 as a starter with 1,719 yards, ten touchdowns and seven interceptions, and he was ten of twenty-three for 119 yards with an interception before getting pulled. Reporting suggests he could be traded before Tuesday’s deadline.

The backup is rookie Carson Beck — the sixty-fifth overall pick in the 2026 draft — who’s dealing with a back injury and is week to week. LaFleur on him: “I just think that’s the right thing to do.”

So understand the matchup properly: the team Drew Lock beat 31-7 had Jacoby Brissett. The team arriving Sunday has a quarterback coming off a fourteen-point comeback win in Dallas.

Arizona is 3-5, they’re one game behind the Rams for third in the NFC West, and they have life for the first time this season. Do not expect 31-7.

Now the run game, and I have to give you the bad number.

Seattle ranks nineteenth in the NFL in rushing at 109.6 yards a game.

And twelves, that figure has gotten worse. Two weeks ago I told you it was 113.7 a game. Eighty-one rushing yards against Chicago dragged it down.

And the part that stings: Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price were both held under thirty yards on Monday night.

That was supposed to be the fix. The healthy backfield arrived and produced nothing — though in fairness, Charbonnet was on a managed snap plan that the scoreboard destroyed, and Macdonald said so: “you can’t ask guys to come back and play a certain amount of snaps... the game shook out a certain way.”

The good news, and it’s real: as of Wednesday, Charbonnet and Price are not on the injury report at all. No designation. Sunday is the first clean look we get at this backfield.

Which brings us to the deadline, and twelves, I have a new thought on this one.

The trade deadline is Tuesday, November tenth.

Here’s the honest state of the Jonathan Taylor story: Indianapolis immediately rejected a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick. Tom Pelissero reported they won’t move him for anything less than “a premium haul” — comparable to the McCaffrey package, which was a second, a third, a fourth and a fifth. And Seattle is no longer on Adam Schefter’s list of teams inquiring. The teams still calling are Green Bay, Minnesota, Denver and the Giants.

The fan version points out — correctly — that Seattle still has its first and second round picks plus projected compensatory picks. But remember the rule I gave you: you cannot trade a compensatory pick before the league awards it. They aren’t assets yet. So the real ammunition is a first and a second, and I am not spending a second-round pick on a running back when my left tackle is hurt and my quarterback has a cracked rib.

But here’s the thought I haven’t heard anybody make.

Jacoby Brissett might be available.

Arizona just demoted him to third string and reporting says he could be moved before Tuesday. He’s a veteran who started eight games this season, thrown for 1,719 yards, and he knows how to walk into a building and run an offense.

Seattle’s quarterback room right now is: a starter with a cracked rib and a quad, a backup with a bad elbow making his second career start for this team, a young player in Jalen Milroe, and a UFL MVP on the practice squad.

If John Schneider is going to spend anything before Tuesday, I’d rather it be a late pick on a veteran quarterback than a premium pick on a fourth running back.

That’s not a report. Nobody has connected Seattle to Brissett. That’s my opinion, clearly labeled — but the logic is sitting right there, and it costs a fraction of what Indianapolis wants.

Now the title, and here’s my verdict.

“DEAL DONE” — technically true. Seattle signed a quarterback. It was a practice-squad signing, announced in a four-move transaction note.

“DREAM QUARTERBACK” — no. Adrian Martinez is the fourth quarterback in the building. He is a genuinely interesting player with a UFL MVP, a championship game MVP and forty-five college rushing touchdowns — and he is insurance, not a solution.

“TO REPLACE SAM DARNOLD” — no. Drew Lock replaces Sam Darnold. That was announced Wednesday and it’s the actual news of the week.

But here’s what I’ll say for the spirit of it: if you’d told me on Monday night that Seattle’s response to losing its quarterback would be to sign the only available arm who already knows a cousin of our offense, I’d have called that a well-run football operation. It’s not a dream. It’s competence, and competence is underrated in November.

Let me close.

Arizona at Lumen Field on Sunday, November eighth. Deadline Tuesday, November tenth.

Here’s the week in one breath: Darnold is out with a cracked rib and a quad. Lock starts with a sore elbow and a 127.3 passer rating. A UFL MVP is on the practice squad because he speaks the language. Charbonnet and Price are finally healthy. Cross, Shaheed and Leonard Williams are all day-to-day. And the opponent just found a quarterback.

And the thing that decides it is the same thing that’s decided every game this season: first down, second down, first down. One for eleven on third down is how you lose by seventeen. Eleven of sixteen is how you beat the Chiefs.

Drew Lock doesn’t need to be Sam Darnold on Sunday. He needs to be on schedule.

Twelves, two questions. First: do you trust Drew Lock? I do, with a caveat — 73 percent, four touchdowns, no interceptions and a 31-7 win over this exact franchise. But that elbow is the whole ballgame, and Friday’s report is the one that matters.
