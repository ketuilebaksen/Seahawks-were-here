AND NOW — the best football answer Mike Macdonald has given in weeks, and twelves, this explains Seattle's entire season on the ground.

A reporter asked a genuinely expert question: "you guys have chosen to MAJOR IN OUTSIDE ZONE. A lot of teams have decided to move more towards DUO. What are the PROS AND CONS of majoring in one versus the other?"

And Macdonald's answer is a clinic. Let me take it piece by piece.

"A lot of the league right now is — we call it MID ZONE — but there's variations. But you're right, WE MAJOR IN WIDE ZONE. THAT'S WHERE WE DECIDED TO HANG OUR HAT."

First, what these plays are, because you need both to follow this.

WIDE ZONE — or outside zone — is the horizontal run. The entire offensive line moves laterally toward the sideline, the back runs parallel to the line, and he's looking for a crease to appear as the defense flows with him. It's a race. You're trying to outrun the defense to the edge and then cut up through whatever opens.

DUO is the opposite philosophy. It's vertical. Two linemen double-team one defender and DRIVE him backward, while the running back waits and reads the linebacker. I covered this weeks ago when Macdonald first mentioned it.

And here's his comparison, and it's beautifully precise:

"WIDE ZONE'S A LITTLE BIT MORE OF A VOLATILE PLAY. There's A LITTLE MORE VARIANCE in wide zone. DUO — it's more of a HAT ON A HAT. Tends to create LESS EXPLOSIVES, I GUESS, ON AVERAGE. Probably the biggest differences."

Twelves, "VOLATILE" and "VARIANCE" are the key words, and this is the most useful thing you'll learn about Seattle's offense all season.
