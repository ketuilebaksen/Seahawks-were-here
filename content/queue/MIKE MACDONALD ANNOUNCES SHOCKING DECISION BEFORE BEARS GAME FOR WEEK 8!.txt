This time there really is a decision, twelves — and it belongs to a man who tore his ACL in the playoffs.

Zach Charbonnet is going to play on Monday night.

Mike Macdonald, at his Friday press conference: “Now we’re at the stage of the plan where he’s going to play, and we have a plan for how much and what types of plays he’s going to have.”

He’s going to play.

Seahawks Nation, Zach Charbonnet tore his ACL in the divisional round of the playoffs. He had surgery on February twentieth. He has not taken a single snap in the 2026 season. He came off the PUP list, practiced fully for the first time, and was still ruled out against Kansas City last week.

And on Monday night, against the Chicago Bears, in prime time at Lumen Field, he is in the lineup.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Seattle is 4-3, defending Super Bowl champions. Chicago is 5-2. Monday November second, 8:15 Eastern, on ABC and ESPN. Seattle favored by three. The final injury report is out, and I have all of it. Let’s go.

First — the thing Macdonald said about him that I want everybody to hear.

Unprompted, in the middle of a different answer, the head coach stopped and did this:

“I just think this is a great opportunity to give a shout out to Zach. To come back on the timetable he is, and to be in the position that he is, and the work ethic that he’s shown, and been able to work through what it takes to get back from an injury like that at this point in time — is remarkable.”

And then the part that got me:

“Zach’s a guy that kind of works in the shadows. He’s already had our respect, but hopefully people understand what kind of stud this guy is to come back on this timetable and be ready to go.”

“Works in the shadows.”

Twelves, think about this man’s year. He got hurt winning a Super Bowl. The team went and lifted the trophy in February, and he had knee surgery on the twentieth of that month. Then he spent eight months in a training room while everybody else got rings — and then he spent seven more weeks on a PUP list watching his team go 4-3 without him while the entire internet screamed that Seattle needed to trade two draft picks for a running back.

And nobody asked him about any of it, because he was in the shadows.

Now his head coach is making sure you know his name.

Now — the plan, and the philosophy behind it, because Macdonald gave away something good here.

He was asked about Charbonnet being limited in Thursday’s practice. The answer:

“It’s just — you have a plan, and then part of the plan is that you leave room to navigate the plan.”

“You leave room to navigate the plan.”

I love that sentence. It’s not a plan if it can’t survive contact with reality. And he applied it straight to Monday night: “We have a plan for how much and what types of plays he’s going to have. And again, that’s probably — it might waver a little bit throughout the game, but that’s okay. We’re sticking to it, and so far we’ve put ourselves in a position where I feel like he’s really confident going into the game.”

So expect a snap count. Expect specific packages. Do not expect twenty carries. This is a managed return, and the fact that Macdonald is telling you so in advance is the opposite of hype — it’s a coach setting expectations honestly before anybody can be disappointed.

And here’s what Seattle is actually getting back, because Macdonald laid it out and it’s more than people think.

Asked about what Charbonnet brings besides running:

“He’s a three-down back. He can catch the ball out of the backfield. He can run a pretty significant route tree out of the backfield. He can run all the different types of runs and all the different types of situations. And his pass protection has been excellent since he’s been here.”

And then the line that matters most on Monday night:

“Zach’s as good as they get blocking out of the backfield.”

Twelves, this is the point.

Remember what we’ve been talking about all month — a running back’s pass protection matters more than his rushing average in a game like this. Chicago is fourth in the NFL in pressure rate. Seattle is starting Josh Jones at left tackle because Charles Cross is out, with Quenton Nelson at left guard after four days in the building and Grey Zabel at right guard for the first time in his professional life.

You know what helps a reshuffled offensive line? A back who can block.

“As good as they get blocking out of the backfield” is not a compliment about a running back. It’s an offensive line acquisition. On the night Seattle most needs help protecting Sam Darnold, they’re getting back the man their head coach calls their best pass-protecting back.

And he gave credit around the room too: “We saw George with some great pickups last week” — that’s George Holani — and “Jadarian’s done that earlier in the season as well. He’s an underrated pass blocker.”

So Seattle’s entire backfield can block. That is not an accident; that is how this staff picks running backs.

Now the mystery of the week: Jadarian Price. And twelves, this exchange was fascinating.

Here’s the setup. Price has no injury designation at all. He’s been on injured reserve for four games with a chest injury, he returned to practice this week, and he is medically fine.

Macdonald on whether he plays: “With JD, I think if we were to make a move, I think that would happen on Monday, to my knowledge. So we’ll see if that’s what we do. We got some things going on roster-wise that we’re working through right now.”

“Some things going on roster-wise.”

Then a reporter nailed it: Just to clarify — Jadarian doesn’t have an injury designation. So if he’s not activated, it’s not injury related. Maybe some other reason?

And Macdonald said: “Yeah, we’re just going to let the transaction speak for itself on that one.”

“Let the transaction speak for itself.”

Twelves, that is a coach telling you, politely, that the answer is on a form and not in his mouth. And here’s the mechanic that explains it.

Activating a player from injured reserve requires a spot on the 53-man roster. Price isn’t hurt — he’s stored. To play him, somebody comes off the 53.

And what did Seattle just do? They traded for Quenton Nelson and waived Christian Haynes to make room. That was the spare spot. It’s gone. Miami claimed Haynes within a day.

So activating Jadarian Price on Monday means another Seahawk loses his place. That’s the “roster-wise” thing they’re working through, and it’s why the coach won’t say the words out loud before the paperwork exists. He is not going to announce who’s getting cut at a Friday press conference.

And that’s what the report means: Seattle has until Monday afternoon to make a move to add Price to the 53. If they don’t, he doesn’t play — and it won’t be because of his chest.

Now the full and final injury report, both teams, because this is the part you actually need.

SEATTLE — OUT: Charles Cross (ankle). Rashid Shaheed (knee). Velus Jones Jr. (hamstring).

On Shaheed, Macdonald said: “He’s doing well. Doing well. Just wasn’t able to make it to the game. We’ll see next week, but he’s done a great job.” And on the timeline: “Right now he’s out this game, and we’ll recalibrate next week.”

So this is week-to-week, not month-to-month. That’s good news dressed as bad news.

DOUBTFUL: Uchenna Nwosu (ankle).

QUESTIONABLE: Anthony Bradford (knee). Drew Lock (right elbow). Brandon Pili (back). Pat O’Connell (toe).

AND THE GOOD NEWS — no game status, cleared to play: Zach Charbonnet (knee). Jadarian Price (chest). Quenton Nelson (knee). Drake Thomas (ankle). Chazz Surratt (calf). Leonard Williams (achilles). Byron Murphy the Second (back). Connor O’Toole (hamstring). Brady Russell (wrist).

Drake Thomas and Chazz Surratt are both back after missing multiple games. That’s two linebackers returning to a defense that held Patrick Mahomes to three points without them.

And Macdonald made a decision there too: “Drake will start the game.”

But he protected Tyrice Knight in the same breath — “Tyrice has played really good football for us. We’ve shown in the past that we’ll put both guys out there at the same time. We’re not hesitant to do that. Right now Drake’s had a great week, so has Tyrice, and we’ll go from there.”

That’s not a demotion. That’s a rotation with a starter named.

And CHICAGO’s report, which contains a gift.

OUT: Kyle Monangai (thumb and toe). That’s Chicago’s running back.

Twelves, do you understand what that does to this game? Seattle has not allowed a hundred-yard rusher in thirty-six consecutive games. The entire blueprint from the Chiefs win was: take away the run, make the quarterback one-dimensional, and let the front four hunt.

Chicago is now trying to run on the best run defense in football without its starting running back, behind a line missing left tackle Braxton Jones, who’s on injured reserve.

That is the game. That is the whole game.

The rest of Chicago’s report: Malik Muhammad (toe) was limited all week and is about a coin flip. D’Marco Jackson (ankle) is questionable but practiced fully. T.J. Edwards (hamstring) and Jack Sanborn (concussion) have no status after full practices. And Colston Loveland (neck) is a full go with a minor issue.

Now some genuinely sad news that Macdonald referenced, and I have to tell you honestly.

In the middle of a question, a reporter noted that “Elijah’s been out for a little bit now.” That’s Elijah Arroyo.

And the reporting is that Elijah Arroyo has undergone surgery and is likely done for the 2026 season.

That’s a tough one. Arroyo’s season is over. And it makes the next part matter more.

Which brings us to Nick Kallerup — and a block on the play that ended the Chiefs game.

Macdonald on the tight end who’s been filling in: “Nick’s someone that’s done a great job when he’s had his opportunities with us, and we expect the same moving forward. He’s a physical player, and I think he’s got probably underrated — underrated ability to catch the ball and move forward.”

And then he got specific, and this is the detail I love:

“The duo play that hit at the end that we scored on — he made a great block on. Wish he got lined up a little quicker, but he made up for it.”

A “duo” play. Let me explain that, because it’s a real scheme and it’s exactly what Seattle needed.

Duo is a double-team run. Two offensive linemen block one defensive lineman — moving him backward together — while the running back reads the linebacker behind it and picks a side. It is the most physical run in football. There’s no finesse in it. You are simply deciding to move a man against his will.

And that’s the play Seattle scored on at the end of the Kansas City game — Emanuel Wilson’s twenty-one-yard touchdown. Not a trick. Not a wide zone cutback. A duo play, with a tight end who got lined up late and still made the block.

Now think about duo with Quenton Nelson in the lineup. A six-time All-Pro guard whose entire reputation is physicality and relentlessness, in a double-team scheme, against a Chicago defense that ranks 26th in run defense DVOA and allows 4.4 yards a carry.

That’s not a hope. That’s arithmetic.

And here’s the best thing Mike Macdonald said all week, and it’s about a label.

He was asked about Josh Jones, who steps in at left tackle. His answer started normally: “Josh will play left tackle. He’s done a good job when he’s gone in there and played. There was a stretch of time last year where we were missing Charles and Josh came in and played really good football for us. That’s what we expect. That’s why we want him here.”

And then: “Shout out to the guys for being able to bring him back, because he’s a really good player.”

He left, and they brought him back. And now he protects Sam Darnold’s blind side in prime time.

But then Macdonald went somewhere I didn’t expect. Asked about what Josh Jones showed late last year, he started talking about labels:

“Having the depth at that position is so critical. We have some situations going around where it’s like, ‘Hey, I’m not quote-unquote a starter. I’m not on the 53’ — all these labels that people put on, which are important, right? Because you get opportunities and things like that, and there’s money involved.”

“But also the importance of: hey, I’m on the team. I’m on the 70. I have a responsibility, and when given the opportunity, I’m going to be ready to rock and help our team win. And what Josh has done for us since he’s been here is a great example of that.”

“I’m on the 70.”

Twelves, that’s fifty-three plus seventeen. The active roster plus the practice squad. Seventy men.

And he said the quiet part out loud: the labels ARE real, because they decide who plays and who gets paid. He didn’t pretend otherwise. But he’s telling seventy men that the number on the depth chart isn’t the number on the door.

Last week Seattle cut a receiver to activate Anthony Bradford, then signed him back to the practice squad. This week a Hall of Famer took Bradford’s job and a third-round pick got claimed by Miami. And on Monday a man who isn’t on the 53 is starting at left tackle.

“I’m on the 70.” That’s the most accurate description of this football team anybody has given all season.

And a word on Grey Zabel, who got moved for somebody better through no fault of his own.

“Grey’s doing a good job. This is life in the NFL. Things happen. And I know for a fact he’s excited, and he’s going to become even a better version of himself and a better football player moving forward. So I’m excited to see where he goes.”

“This is life in the NFL.” Four words, no sugar. Same as what he said about Bradford: no way to sugarcoat it.

And Quenton Nelson, four days in.

“Pretty much on brand of how you expect it to go. It’s pretty cool — it’s great to see him in Seahawk gear.”

On the emotional arc: “Right now there’s a little bit of like a shock that, hey, this happened. And then I think now you’re feeling really the excitement from both parties, of understanding how much it’s going to make our club better.” Plus: “It’s way more new for him than it is for us.” And: “You can feel the poise, the confidence.”

And then a line that should get your attention, because Nelson is on an expiring contract: “We’re a few days in, hopefully for a longer relationship.”

“Hopefully for a longer relationship.” That’s a head coach saying out loud that he’d like to keep him past this season.

And here’s the smartest thing he said about the line: “Our coaches did a great job of calibrating what’s an easy transition for Quenton, what’s kind of a stretch, how much of the stretch do we want to go for this week — and finding the right medium between what we can go out and nail and execute, versus what we already want to be able to do.”

They trimmed the playbook to fit the new guy. Not learn everything in five days. Find the overlap, master it, add later. That’s how you avoid a disaster in prime time.

He was honest about the risk too: “We’ll have to figure out some solutions as the game goes on, going against a really good team. And that’s par for the course.”

And the message to the team: “This is Friday for us. There’s still time before the game where our focus should shift to really dialing in the details, and making sure our mind, body, spirits are in the best spot going into the game.”

Plus the urgency: “We’re up against the ropes a little bit. We recalibrate. We have a solid game, pull out a win at home, which is awesome — but it’s time to go. It’s time to step our game forward, and there’s a sense of urgency to grow.”

“Up against the ropes a little bit.” After beating the best team in football by seventeen. That’s a coach refusing to let one night become the story.

Now the title, and this week I’m giving it full marks.

Mike Macdonald did announce a decision before the Bears game, and it is genuinely significant: Zach Charbonnet plays Monday night, eight months after ACL surgery, with a managed snap count. Plus Drake Thomas starts, Josh Jones is the left tackle, Shaheed and Cross are out, and Jadarian Price’s availability depends on a roster move nobody will describe yet.

That’s five decisions. The title’s only sin is the word “shocking” — these are considered, explained, and honest.

My pick stays Seahawks 24, Bears 20. Seattle runs duo at the 26th-ranked run defense in football, Charbonnet protects on third down, Josh Jones holds up, and a defense that’s first in the NFL in opponent yards per play faces a Chicago offense without its starting running back. A man tore his ACL in the divisional round, had surgery in February, watched eight months of his own team’s season go by in a training room, and stayed so quiet about it that his head coach had to stop a press conference to tell everybody he exists.
