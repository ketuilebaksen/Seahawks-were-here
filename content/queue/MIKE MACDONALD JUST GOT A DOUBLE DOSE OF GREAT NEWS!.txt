NOW — the roster move nobody is talking about, and I want to flag it.

TREVON DIGGS WAS ELEVATED FROM THE PRACTICE SQUAD, giving Seattle extra depth in the secondary.

And twelves, let me explain what an elevation actually is, because people confuse it with a signing.

A practice squad elevation is temporary. You get TWO per game, and a player can only be elevated THREE TIMES per season. After the game he reverts to the practice squad automatically — no waivers, no risk. It’s how you add a body for one Sunday without using a spot on your fifty-three.

So Seattle used one of its elevations on secondary depth. And the broader point is the right one: “the staff has been AGGRESSIVE about finding contributors wherever they can. Injuries have forced some CREATIVITY throughout the year, but the depth is RESPONDING in a way that keeps this team competitive instead of COLLAPSING when key pieces go down.”

That’s “the power of our seventy,” which Macdonald has now said three weeks running — and which Drew Lock repeated unprompted: “the READY SQUAD — it’s seventy guys ready to roll at any time.”
