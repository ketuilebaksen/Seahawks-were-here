“I don’t care about the score.”

That’s Mike Macdonald, standing at a podium after a seventeen to three win, telling you the scoreboard wasn’t the point.

“I just want us to go let it loose and go be us and feel like us. And a lot of times we felt that way.”

Seahawks Nation — they felt like themselves again. Seventeen to three. Five and four. And they did it without Sam Darnold, without Charles Cross, without Quenton Nelson, without Leonard Williams, without Rashid Shaheed and without Uchenna Nwosu.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. And before anything else: I told you Seahawks twenty-three, Cardinals sixteen. It was seventeen to three. I’ll take being wrong in that direction every single week of my life.

Now, I’ll be straight with you about this title the way I always am: there is no shocking decision in this press conference. But there IS a genuine piece of breaking news in it that changes Seattle’s entire week — and it’s about Drew Lock’s ribs. Let’s get to all of it.

First, Macdonald’s opening statement, because the theme of this entire win is in it.

“Proud of our team. A lot of noise this week with injuries and other things going on. So just the poise that we showed — the twelve was one. We said this game was like the power of our seventy, you know, with the depth we were going to lean on, with the bottom of our roster. And guys did a great job of stepping in. Not flinching.”

“The power of our seventy.”

Twelves, let me explain that phrase, because it’s the whole story of Sunday.

A team doesn’t have fifty-three players. It has seventy. Fifty-three on the active roster, sixteen on the practice squad, plus an international pathway player. Macdonald has used that number all season — “I’m on the seventy” — because the message is that every one of those men is part of this.

And on Sunday he cashed that in. Six starters out. And he went into a division game telling his roster that the bottom of it was going to decide the thing.

It did.

He named them: “Drew Lock, AB coming back, Dante Fowler, eager to see what Riley did — Riley had more of an opportunity, that was cool. Mike Morris.”

Anthony Bradford. The man I spent a whole video telling you not to trade. Back at right guard, in the starting lineup, in a shutout-level win.

And then the stat that matters most, and he led with it himself.

“Got a couple red zone stops, which is a first for us. And then a couple red zone scores. So that was great.”

“Which is a first for us.”

Twelves, stop right there.

Four days ago, Julian Love stood at this same podium and said — his word — that Seattle’s red zone efficiency was “terrible right now.” Mike Macdonald said the same thing in his own press conference: “defensively we get to the red zone and we don’t execute as well.”

And on Sunday they got red zone stops for the first time all season. And scored red zone touchdowns.

That is the single most encouraging thing about this football game. Not the final score. The fact that the two problems this team named in public six days ago were the two problems it fixed on Sunday.

That’s what a well-coached team looks like. You name it on Wednesday, you fix it on Sunday.

And he closed the opening with: “to be able to close the game out in a tough situation there at the goal line, and then our offense putting a four-minute drive together — some really good situational football by our team.”

A goal line stand and a four-minute drive. Those are the two hardest situations in football and Seattle won both of them on the same afternoon.

NOW — the actual news, and this is the headline nobody else is leading with.

Asked what it took for Drew Lock to play through the elbow, Macdonald said: “Yeah, he’s a beast. He just threw — not around, just kind of went after it. And I think our medical people deserve a shout out too, for putting a great plan together.”

“He just went after it.” The man said he was taking every rep on Wednesday, and then he went out and took every snap.

But then a reporter asked why Lock had been looked at in the medical tent.

And Macdonald said: “We’re looking at him for an oblique. So we’ll assess it and go from there.”

An oblique.

A reporter followed up — playing through that as well as the elbow?

“Yeah, exactly. Apparently something with the midsection of our quarterbacks is an issue right now.”

Twelves. Read that again.

Sam Darnold is out with a cracked rib. And Drew Lock finished this football game being evaluated for an oblique injury.

Both of Seattle’s quarterbacks are hurt in the torso.

And Macdonald’s dry joke — “apparently something with the midsection of our quarterbacks is an issue right now” — is funny for about two seconds, and then you realize what it actually means for this week.

Because that’s exactly where he took it later in the press conference. Asked whether he has an early sense of Sam Darnold’s chances this week:

“I don’t — I think we’ll probably re-evaluate earlier in the week. But I’m assuming we’re probably going to have to make a decision on who we’re going to get ready and how we’re going to do that. Drew’s situation, we’ll have to evaluate that as well. So it seems like it’s kind of up in the air right now with Drew.”

“We’re probably going to have to make a decision on who we’re going to get ready.”

THERE is your decision, twelves. That’s the real story in this press conference.

Seattle plays at the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday, and right now its head coach cannot tell you which quarterback is starting. Darnold is being re-evaluated with a cracked rib and a quad. Lock is “up in the air” with an elbow and now an oblique. And Jalen Milroe — who has still never thrown a pass in an NFL game — just took a live snap and operated this offense in a division game.

Macdonald on that, and it’s an important detail people are skipping:

“And Jalen Milroe too — like, operating our offense when he took the snap that he took, and being ready to play in case Drew couldn’t go or couldn’t finish. So really a great team effort.”

Milroe got on the field. In a real game. In a real situation. And remember what Drew Lock said about that on Wednesday: “reps for quarterbacks is better than any tape they can watch.” Jalen Milroe just banked one.

And then Macdonald said this about both of them:

“Really, both quarterbacks that have played — I mean, talk about, I don’t know how you get any tougher. Both guys tough as nails.”

“I don’t know how you get any tougher.”

One played four quarters on a cracked rib and didn’t tell anybody. The other played a division game on a bad elbow and came off being checked for an oblique. He’s right. You don’t get any tougher than that.

Now the running backs, and this is the development we’ve been waiting on for eight months.

Asked about Zach Charbonnet helping close the game out and Jadarian Price making plays in the passing game:

“Yeah, it’s great. Really happy for Zach. Happy for JD. Definitely felt Zach in his power, and the ability to break tackles and own the sideline there — even though we got the penalty to move us back. But I’m really just happy for him, just to put himself in that position. And I think they should continue to get better with more reps and more experience.”

“Definitely felt Zach in his power.”

Twelves, think about where we were four days ago. Macdonald told us Zach Charbonnet “hasn’t had a live rep since the injury” — not one, since he tore his ACL in January. He played Monday night for the first time in eight months and got held under thirty yards.

And on Sunday he closed out a division game with his power.

“Own the sideline there.” That’s a back finishing a run by lowering his shoulder and refusing to go down in bounds. That’s the Zach Charbonnet we remember, and it took exactly one week.

And on Jadarian Price, Macdonald gave the best football explanation of the whole press conference.

Asked about Price’s day and the big third-down catch on the first scoring series:

“Yeah, I felt him out there. I thought he was decisive with his runs. Sometimes he’s the primary in some of those pass concepts. Sometimes he’s kind of at the end of the progression, and when the ball finds him — if you can make a guy miss, that’s a great example. It doesn’t have to be some extraordinary play to create explosives. Let’s just be efficient with our decision-making and get it in our players’ hands and let them do the rest.”

“It doesn’t have to be some extraordinary play to create explosives.”

That is the thesis of this entire offense and I want to make sure you catch it.

Remember what Sam Darnold said two weeks ago? “You’re not ever really seeking out explosive plays.” And Macdonald just said the same thing from the coaching chair. The explosive play is not a sixty-yard bomb. The explosive play is a five-yard checkdown to your running back who then makes one defender miss.

You don’t hunt explosives. You create them by being efficient and letting good players be good.

And the forward look: “He’s just going to continue to get better now that he’s stacking weeks and he’s getting healthier.”

“Stacking weeks.” Remember, Macdonald told us Price’s snap constraints were “lessened” this week. You just watched what lessened constraints look like.

Now the offensive line, and this is quietly remarkable.

Asked how the line played and whether it was in sync:

“Yeah, I thought — we still ended up in some longer situations, where those are inevitable to some degree, but we want to minimize those. But I thought the guys did a good job. Be fun to watch the tape. Always room for improvement, but with the movement that we’ve had across our line in the last two weeks, to kind of come together and especially finish the game the way they did was really cool.”

“With the movement that we’ve had across our line in the last two weeks.”

Twelves, let’s count that movement. Two weeks ago Seattle traded for Quenton Nelson and moved Grey Zabel to right guard. Then Nelson needed a knee procedure. Then Zabel went back to left guard and Anthony Bradford came back to right guard. And they’ve been without Charles Cross at left tackle the whole time.

That is three different interior configurations in fourteen days, with the starting left tackle unavailable.

And they didn’t allow a sack.

Which connects to the pressure question. A reporter noted Arizona got pressure early and then didn’t end up with a sack. Why the improvement?

“Yeah, kind of both. A lot of times teams give you a look that you might not have seen, but our rules should be able to handle those things. And sometimes there will be free runners and we have to get rid of the ball. Speaks to our team just making the adjustments and moving forward. But we want to be on our game to start games, for sure. So we’ll look at that and try to come up with a solution.”

“Our rules should be able to handle those things.”

That’s the honest version of pass protection. You don’t have a call for every look. You have rules — and when an unscouted blitz shows up, the rules have to hold. On Sunday they bent early, then held all afternoon.

But notice he still wouldn’t let it go: “we want to be on our game to START games.” The slow-start problem is still on his list. Even in a shutout-level win.

Now the defense, and this answer is about character more than scheme.

Asked about the defense after a long opening drive and a score:

“Yeah, we were just talking to Leo about it. Just the composure of our team. There’s several things that didn’t go our way today. I felt like we got the ball out several times. There’s some penalties against us to kind of put us in some not-great situations. But just no flinch from the fellas. I thought our coaches did a great job of finding solutions and staying calm in the moment, even though it gets kind of crazy sometimes. But you could feel it from our group that they were determined to get that stop at the end of the game, and we were confident we could pull it off.”

“No flinch from the fellas.”

And he was talking to Leonard Williams about it — the man who missed his first game since Week 4 of 2024. He was there. He just couldn’t play.

And “determined to get that stop at the end of the game” is the goal line stand. Arizona at the goal line, Seattle holding. That’s where this game ended.

Now the best story of the day, and it’s a lesson in leadership.

A reporter reminded Macdonald that DeMarcus Lawrence had said this week that he felt like he was part of the red zone problem — specifically his C gap — and then he made a tackle for loss at the end of this game. What does that say about him?

And Macdonald’s answer:

“Yeah, I didn’t realize that Daw had said that until Dave filled me in later on in the week. But he’s just like a great example of the guys on our team. And for him to be a leader and to show the example of — look, our falling short doesn’t fall on Daw’s shoulders. Everybody knows that. But for someone of that kind of pedigree to take that type of responsibility and accountability is pretty special. So he’s awesome. Played great today, and we definitely follow his lead.”

Twelves, follow this chain all the way through, because it’s beautiful.

Seattle has a red zone problem. A thirteen-year veteran with over sixty career sacks stands up and says it’s partly his fault — his specific gap. His head coach says plainly it wasn’t on him and everybody knows that.

And then on Sunday, Seattle gets its first red zone stops of the season — and DeMarcus Lawrence makes a tackle for loss at the end of the game.

“We definitely follow his lead.”

That’s how a locker room actually works. Not a speech. A man with nothing to prove taking blame he didn’t owe, and then going and fixing it on the field.

And remember Julian Love’s line from Wednesday: “a lesser team would be pointing fingers, turning on each other.” This is the proof. Their most decorated defender pointed the finger at himself.

Now the play of the game, and the coaching decision behind it.

Asked about Nick Emmanwori’s play on Trey McBride on fourth down:

“Yeah, it was a big-time play. We put him on McBride and felt confidently he could cover him. And he did. So that was pretty cool.”

And then a reporter noted Emmanwori was on McBride on a lot of third downs too, when the ball didn’t go that way.

And here’s the coaching reveal: “Yeah, we didn’t rush Nick as much this week. Different reasons for why we did that, but it just shows you — hey, wherever you put your focus.”

“We didn’t rush Nick as much this week.”

Twelves, that’s a real tactical decision and I want to explain it.

Nick Emmanwori has been used as a blitzer — a 6'3", 227-pound safety who comes off the edge. And Macdonald took that away this week and used him as a cover man on Arizona’s best offensive player instead.

Trey McBride is one of the two or three best tight ends in football. And Seattle’s answer was: put the rookie safety on him and let him erase him. On third down after third down the ball didn’t go there. And on fourth down, Emmanwori won the play.

That’s a defensive coordinator looking at an opponent, identifying the one man who can beat him, and assigning a specific player to delete him. And it worked.

And Macdonald’s praise was about process, not talent: “he really had a good week of focusing in on details, and playing his style of ball but also being detail oriented and being on his game. So I’m glad he had the production to back that up.”

“Glad he had the production to back that up.” He prepared right and got paid for it.

Now AJ Barner — two touchdowns, and this one’s personal.

“Yeah, proud of AJ. We love him and we trust him and we understand what type of player he is. So we can kind of feel the frustration with him, of what he expects from himself. But just no flinch, keep attacking it. He’s a leader on our offense and he provides so much great energy. So when he’s on his game, it really helps our offense.”

And the tactical part: “to get into some of those short-yardage situations where we can run the sneak is kind of galvanizing, energizes the group — and then to put the ball away in the red zone when they take Jax away, to have another option down there, is really helpful.”

“When they take Jax away.”

THAT is the sentence, twelves.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba carries the highest target share in the National Football League. Every defense Seattle plays from here on out is going to roll coverage to him in the red zone. It’s the obvious plan.

And on Sunday Arizona did exactly that — and AJ Barner caught two touchdowns.

That’s the answer to the problem everyone’s been asking about. Cooper Kupp said it last week: “you’ve got to compete without the ball.” And the flip side is that when they take your best player away, somebody else has to be ready. Barner was. Twice.

And he played through a hand injury to do it.

Now Macdonald’s grade on the whole thing.

Asked how this game graded for playing Seahawks football:

“Yeah, I thought we took a step today. A lot of positive things. Being able to close games out like that was great. I thought we had some great situational stuff at the end of the half, where you’re continually putting pressure on our opponent. And there’s some things we fell short at, that of course we’re going to go back and attack and try to figure out ways to move forward. But really, just like I told the guys — I don’t care about the score. I just want us to go let it loose and go be us and feel like us. And a lot of times we felt that way. So that’s a positive step for us.”

“I don’t care about the score. I just want us to feel like us.”

Twelves, that is the exact answer to Julian Love’s problem.

On Wednesday, Julian Love said: “we’re still in identity-finding mode... right now it does not feel how it’s supposed to look.”

And on Sunday his head coach said: “a lot of times we felt that way.”

That’s the identity showing up. Not finished. Not perfect. But present.

And the honest part — the penalties. Macdonald was asked about three calls that went against Seattle: the challenge on the sideline catch, the late hit called on Jalen Sundell, and the forward progress ruling on the fumble.

His answer: “Well, yeah, I think those just didn’t go our way. They saw Jalen as a late hit. I think they went to New York on the challenge — I think they said it was part of like a toe drag.”

“Those just didn’t go our way.” No tirade. No conspiracy. He explained the rulings and moved on. And remember, he’d already said the penalties “put us in some not-great situations” and the team didn’t flinch anyway.

Let me bring it home.

Here’s where Seattle stands tonight. Five and four. A division win. The first red zone stops of the season. A goal line stand. A four-minute drive. No sacks allowed behind a line that’s changed three times in two weeks. Zach Charbonnet running with power again. Jadarian Price stacking weeks. AJ Barner with two touchdowns when they took Jaxon Smith-Njigba away. Nick Emmanwori erasing Trey McBride. And DeMarcus Lawrence making the tackle for loss on a problem he’d publicly put on his own shoulders.

And here’s the week ahead: at the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday, then the bye in Week 11.

With a quarterback decision coming that Mike Macdonald himself says is up in the air. Darnold re-evaluated early in the week with a cracked rib. Lock being looked at for an oblique on top of the elbow. And Jalen Milroe, who now has a live NFL snap, standing right behind them.
