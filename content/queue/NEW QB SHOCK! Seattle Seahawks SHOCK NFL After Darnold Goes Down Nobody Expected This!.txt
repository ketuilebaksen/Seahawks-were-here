Now let me get to the part I really wanted to talk about today.

We all say "defend the crown." But do you know how hard that actually is?

Since the 2004 New England Patriots went back-to-back, exactly one team in the entire National Football League has repeated as Super Bowl champion — the Kansas City Chiefs. One. In two decades. Teams win a title, and then something always gets them: the salary cap, the injuries, the twenty-two other teams who now circle you on the schedule, the emotional drop after the parade.

Let me give you the whole list, because it's shorter than you think. In the entire Super Bowl era, only nine franchises have ever gone back-to-back: the Packers, the Dolphins, the Steelers twice, the 49ers, the Cowboys, the Broncos, the Patriots, and the Chiefs. That's it. That's sixty seasons of professional football and nine repeats.

And Seahawks fans know this story personally. Seattle won it all after the 2013 season, went right back to the Super Bowl the following year — and lost it on the one-yard line. We were one play away from being on that list. Some of you still can't watch that highlight. I know I can't.

So when I say this is hard, I'm not being dramatic. This franchise has been exactly here before and came up one yard short.

And that's why the way Seattle has opened this season matters more than the scores suggest.

Because look at what's already been thrown at this team. They lost their Super Bowl-winning quarterback on the fifth play of the season. They lost their most versatile defender to offseason ankle surgery. Their right guard just went on IR after twenty-one straight starts. Their starting safety Ty Okada hasn't played a snap. Their nose tackle is in concussion protocol. Their rookie running back got banged up. And their offensive coordinator is calling plays in the NFL for the first time in his life.

That's the "championship hangover" script. That's exactly how title defenses fall apart.

Seattle is 2-0 with a twelve-game winning streak — the longest in franchise history.

And it's not fluky. In Arizona they outgained the Cardinals 381 to 151. That's a 230-yard difference on the road in a division game. They've allowed seventeen points total across two weeks. They finished that game with a sixteen-play, seventy-yard drive that ate more than ten minutes and ended with AJ Barner on a tush push.

That's not a team hanging on. That's a team dictating.

And here's what I keep coming back to. The reason repeats are so rare is that championship teams get brittle. One injury and the whole thing wobbles. What Seattle has shown in two weeks is the opposite of brittle. Quarterback goes down? The backup throws four touchdowns and no picks. Right guard goes on IR? A third-year player makes his first career start and the offense puts up 31. Safety needs a snap count? A practice squad call-up tips the pass that wins the opener.

That's not depth as a buzzword. That's depth that actually shows up on Sundays.

There's a roster-building piece to this too, and it's the reason I think this window doesn't close quickly. Look at who Seattle is built around. Jaxon Smith-Njigba is 24 years old and the reigning Offensive Player of the Year. Devon Witherspoon just signed a four-year extension. Nick Emmanwori is in year two. Jadarian Price was a first-round pick this spring. And Mike Macdonald is still in his thirties, three seasons into being a head coach.

Most champions are old. Most champions repeat-attempt with a roster that's about to get expensive and start breaking down. Seattle's core is young and mostly locked up. That's the difference between a one-year party and an actual era.
