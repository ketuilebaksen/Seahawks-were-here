This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Let's go.

First, the correction, because I owe you that before anything else.

On Sunday I told you Charbonnet's four-game reserve-PUP requirement expires and that the earliest he could play was Week 5 — San Francisco, at Lumen Field, the same team he got hurt against in January. All of that is factually correct.

What I implied was that he'd be out there. Mike Macdonald shut that down.

Zach Charbonnet will not play against the 49ers.

But here's the thing — the actual update is genuinely encouraging, and I'd rather have this version than a rushed debut. This is his second week of practice, and this week he gets team reps for the first time since the surgery. Not individual rehab work on a side field. Real practice, against real bodies.

And listen to how his head coach described it: "I think we're off to a great start. He's going to get team reps this week, which is really exciting... he looks pretty dang good."

He looks pretty dang good. From a coach who has been careful with every injury timeline all season. Macdonald also noted the rehab has gotten him to the point where he can do the dynamic stuff — cutting, shifting, changing direction. That's the part that actually matters with an ACL. Running in a straight line is week eight of rehab. Planting a foot and cutting is the real test.

So here's the honest picture: he's on schedule, he looks good, he's practicing with the team, and he's not playing Sunday. And the clock is still ticking — Seattle opened his twenty-one day window on October 1st, which means they must add him to the active roster by October 22nd or lose him for the entire season.

Three weeks. That's the real deadline.

Now the bad news, and this one changes the shape of the whole week.

Jadarian Price was placed on injured reserve on Saturday.

He aggravated the chest injury he first picked up in Arizona, didn't practice Thursday, got ruled out for the Chargers game, and then went on IR. That means a minimum of four games — he's gone for at least three more weeks after Sunday.

Seattle's first-round pick, thirty-second overall, is on injured reserve in October.

And I want to read you what Macdonald said about him, because this staff keeps doing the same thing and it keeps impressing me: "He's a tough son of a gun, and mentally, for being a young player, he's handling everything really well."

Think about this kid's month. Two fumbles in three games. Benched mid-game in Washington. Brought back after halftime. Then the chest injury flares and he's on IR. That is a brutal introduction to professional football — and his head coach is still standing at a podium publicly defending his character.

That's the same man who said "we're going to continue to give him the ball, we trust him" after the fumbles. And it's the exact lesson Macdonald says he learned from the Harbaugh brothers: "They love their players. They have their players' backs at all times."

Price will be fine. He's twenty-two and he's going to be good. But he's not going to be good this month.

So count the backfield. Price on IR. Charbonnet not playing. George Holani working through a rib.

Seattle's running backs on Sunday, against a 4-0 team, are Emanuel Wilson and George Holani — both of them undrafted free agents.

And then there's the third piece of news, which might be the most important of all.

Leonard Williams hurt his ankle in the fourth quarter on Sunday.

He walked off the field under his own power but with a limp, and he had additional testing after the game. Macdonald's words: "We'll see. It's an ankle. I'd say we're optimistic for the game, but we've got to see how the week goes."

Optimistic. But we've got to see how the week goes.

Now let me tell you why that sentence should worry you more than it sounds, and it's one number: since Seattle traded for him from the Giants in 2023, Leonard Williams has missed exactly one football game. One. In three seasons. That was a 2024 game against Detroit.

This man does not miss games. So when there's any uncertainty at all about him, you pay attention.

And look at what he is to this defense. He's the anchor of the interior. He leads this team in sacks. He's the one who named the defense — "The Dark Side." He's the guy who said of this pass rush, "you see guys from every level raising their hand, and I think that's pretty rare." On Sunday the interior pressure was the whole reason Justin Herbert got sacked four times for thirty-five yards.

Take him out and Seattle's front has to beat San Francisco's offensive line with Byron Murphy the Second and Jarran Reed — who, by the way, has been working through a foot issue of his own.

So that's the real injury picture heading into the biggest game of the year: running back is in pieces, and the best interior defender is a maybe.

Which brings me to the thing people have been asking me about all week.

Should Seattle go sign a veteran safety? Specifically — should Seattle go get Harrison Smith?

And as always: this is not a report. Nobody has said this. No insider, no source, nothing. There is not one reported conversation between Seattle and anybody about Harrison Smith. Zero. This is me, sitting here, looking at a situation and letting my imagination run.

Now let me give you the facts, because the numbers getting passed around on this one are garbled beyond recognition. I've seen figures that make no sense at all.

Here's the real file. Harrison Smith was a first-round pick in 2012, twenty-ninth overall, by Minnesota. He is thirty-seven years old. He has made six Pro Bowls — five in a row from 2015 through 2019, plus another in 2021. He was first-team All-Pro in 2017 and second-team in 2018. And he has thirty-nine career interceptions, which is a genuinely elite number for a safety.

That is a tremendous football player and a near-certain Hall of Fame conversation at his position.

And here's the part that ends this: he re-signed with the Minnesota Vikings on a one-year deal worth about twelve and a quarter million dollars, for his fifteenth NFL season.

Fifteenth season. With the only franchise he's ever played for. On a brand new contract he agreed to this year.

That is not a player who gets traded in October. A thirty-seven-year-old who chose to come back for one more year with his own team, who has been the voice of that defense for over a decade, is not getting shipped across the country at the deadline. And Minnesota just created a roster spot specifically to bring him back.

The door isn't just closed. Nobody ever opened it.

But I want to give the underlying argument its fair hearing, because it isn't stupid.

The case goes like this: Seattle just committed fourteen penalties for a franchise-record one hundred and fifty yards. A veteran communicator in the back end reduces miscommunication, cleans up pre-snap confusion, and brings a calming voice to a young secondary. If the problem is discipline, get a disciplined veteran.

I understand it. And here's why I think it's the wrong diagnosis.

The penalties weren't communication problems. They were composure problems.

Seven of those fourteen flags were personal fouls. Seven. Macdonald's exact words: "To have seven personal foul penalties is unacceptable on any level, however you slice it. So that's not us, and that's not going to be how we play football around here."

A personal foul is not a coverage bust. It's not a safety and a corner reading the same route differently. It's a decision a grown man makes after the whistle — a late hit, a shove, a hand in a facemask out of frustration. You don't sign a thirty-seven-year-old safety to fix that. You fix that in a meeting room on Wednesday.

And here's the second reason, which is the bigger one: safety is the single deepest position on this roster right now.

Look at it honestly. Julian Love played Sunday with no injury designation at all — fully cleared, and he's the communicator, the man who calls himself the glue of this defense, with interceptions in back-to-back weeks earlier this year. Nick Emmanwori played his first game completely off the leash and put up nine tackles, two tackles for loss, a sack and a pass defensed — the six-three, two-twenty, 4.38 chess piece his own head coach describes with "we've never really had a player like him, so we are kind of making it up as we go." Ty Okada is back at practice after missing the first three games. AJ Finley started a game in Washington on two days' notice and Macdonald said the back end was organized. And Rodney Thomas the Second went from practice squad call-up to tipping the pass that won the season opener to a permanent roster spot.

That's five safeties, three of whom have started an NFL game this season, with the youngest of them being the most unique defender on the team.

So the question isn't "would Harrison Smith help." Of course he would — he's a six-time Pro Bowler. The question is: whose snaps does he take?

And the answer is Nick Emmanwori's. The twenty-three-year-old whose snap count has climbed zero, nine, thirty-six — and who just had his best game as a pro in the first week nobody limited him.

You don't take reps away from that. You feed that.

So here's my verdict on the whole idea: no. Not because Harrison Smith isn't great. Because he isn't available, and because safety is the one position on this roster where Seattle genuinely needs nothing.

And if you want the broader lesson, it's the same one I've landed on four times in a week now — with Kayvon Thibodeaux, with Quenton Nelson, with Kyle Pitts, and now with Harrison Smith.

The players you want are the players nobody sells. The Giants told teams they had no interest in trading Thibodeaux. The Colts can franchise tag Nelson. Atlanta extended Pitts in June with thirty-six million guaranteed. Minnesota just re-signed Smith for season fifteen.

Four names. Four closed doors. And every single time, the real answer turned out to be somebody already in the building.

But — and here's where I'll be honest with you — this week is the first week I actually do see a hole worth a phone call.

It isn't safety. It's running back.

Price is on IR for at least three more weeks. Charbonnet can't play Sunday and has to be activated by October 22nd. Holani has a rib. Seattle is going into a game against a 4-0 division leader with two undrafted running backs, one of whom is banged up.

And the thing is, Emanuel Wilson has been genuinely excellent — twenty-one carries for eighty-one yards plus thirty-nine receiving and two touchdowns against the Chargers, after twenty-one carries for ninety-one yards in Arizona. He's carried this backfield for two straight weeks and he deserves every bit of credit. But asking an undrafted back to be the entire run game against San Francisco, Denver and Kansas City over the next three weeks is a lot.

Seattle doesn't need a star. They need a body who can take ten carries. And unlike every other name on this list, that player actually exists on the market — veteran running backs are the cheapest commodity in football. Remember, Seattle brought in Dontae McMillan for a visit back in September, a thousand-yard back out of Eastern Michigan who signed with Baltimore as an undrafted free agent.

That's the move I'd make this week. Not a six-time Pro Bowl safety. A fourth running back.
