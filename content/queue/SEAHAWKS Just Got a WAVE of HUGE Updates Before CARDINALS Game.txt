UPDATE ONE: Seattle has a new quarterback.

His name is Adrian Martinez, and he has been signed to the practice squad.

Now, I’ve seen this signing described as “he’s with the Jets,” and I want to clean that up, because the actual résumé is more interesting than the shrug it’s getting.

Adrian Martinez was the starting quarterback at Nebraska as a true freshman. He threw for 8,491 yards there. Then he transferred to Kansas State, went six and three as a starter, ran for 627 yards and ten touchdowns, and that team won the Big 12 Championship.

His career college totals: 9,752 passing yards, 51 passing touchdowns — and forty-five rushing touchdowns.

Forty-five. That is not a quarterback number. That is a running back number attached to a quarterback.

Then in 2024 he went to the United Football League and played for the Birmingham Stallions, and what he did there is remarkable. He completed 134 of 229 passes for 1,748 yards, fifteen touchdowns and three interceptions. He also rushed for 528 yards — which led the entire league. Not led the quarterbacks. Led the league.

He was named UFL Most Valuable Player. Then he won the championship game and was named Championship Game MVP too.

Now the honest part, because I’m not going to oversell a practice squad quarterback to you.

His NFL career is one game. Detroit signed him undrafted and waived him. He spent time on the Jets practice squad. He was with the 49ers last season, got promoted in October, played exactly one game — against the Giants, where he took a knee on the final play — and was released in August of this year.

One NFL game. One rushing attempt. Minus one yard.

So no, this is not a savior. This is a practice squad quarterback.

But here’s why I like it anyway: he is not a game manager. There’s a type of quarterback whose ceiling you can see immediately — hand it off, don’t make mistakes. That is not this guy. Forty-five rushing touchdowns in college and a league rushing title in the UFL tells you there’s a real athlete in there. If this season ever reaches the point where Seattle needs a fourth quarterback to make a play, you’d rather it be a dual-threat MVP than a clipboard holder.

UPDATE TWO: why this signing actually happened — and it’s about Thursday, not Sunday.

Let me lay out Seattle’s quarterback room as of Wednesday morning.

Sam Darnold: out. Drew Lock: limited two days in a row with a throwing elbow, and his own head coach said, and I quote, “he’s not going to take 8,000 throws.” Jalen Milroe: has never thrown a pass in an NFL game.

Hurt. Hurt. And never done it.

That’s a practice problem before it is ever a Sunday problem. You still have to run a full week of preparation. Somebody has to throw the football at practice while Lock’s reps are being counted — and you are not going to burn your rookie’s whole developmental week standing in as a scout team arm.

And understand the mechanics: Martinez is on the practice squad, not the fifty-three. For him to play Sunday, Seattle would have to elevate him — two elevations per game, three per player per season. Practice squad money is about $13,750 a week at the floor. So this costs almost nothing, commits to nothing, and solves the actual problem: getting through Thursday and Friday without asking a sore elbow to carry the whole practice.

And one detail I like: Martinez was in San Francisco. Seattle’s offense and San Francisco’s offense come from the same coaching family. He already speaks a dialect of this language.

UPDATE THREE: the second signing — and there’s a story behind it that broke twenty-four hours later.

Seattle also brought back outside linebacker Jamie Sheriff to the practice squad.

And the read on that, reasonably, was: this isn’t a reaction to anybody being hurt, it’s just depth at a position where bodies break down. Edge rusher is a collision job.

On Wednesday, that was true. Then Thursday happened.

Derick Hall was added to the injury report with a back injury and did not practice.

Derick Hall. One of your primary edge rushers. New name, new injury, no practice. And depending on which outlet you read, Uchenna Nwosu either stayed limited or was downgraded to not practicing at all — the reports genuinely conflict, and I’ll be straight with you that I can’t resolve it, but either way he is on that report with an ankle.

So on Wednesday Seattle quietly added an outside linebacker to the practice squad, and on Thursday the outside linebacker room got a brand-new injury and a question mark.

That is not luck, twelves. That is what a well-run roster looks like from the outside. You don’t add depth because somebody got hurt. You add it because somebody is about to.

UPDATE FOUR: the Darnold picture, with the number nobody wants to say out loud.

Here’s the official word: Darnold is out, he’s week to week, and he is not going on injured reserve. That last part matters, because IR means a minimum of four games. They expect him back sooner than that.

And the reason he’s out: a quad injury from his final drive on Monday — and a cracked rib he suffered on the second play of that game and then played all four quarters without telling his head coach. Macdonald only found out after. “He’s tough as nails.”

Now the uncomfortable part, and I’m going to say it honestly because this channel doesn’t do propaganda.

Sam Darnold this season: 110 of 177, 62.1 percent, 1,290 yards, eight touchdowns — and eight interceptions. An 80.5 passer rating.

Eight and eight.

I’ve heard that called a dumpster fire. I won’t use that phrase, because the man was playing on a cracked rib four days ago and he’s earned better than that from his own fanbase. But I’m not going to pretend eight-and-eight with an eighty rating is fine, because it isn’t. That’s a one-to-one touchdown-to-interception ratio from the quarterback who’s supposed to be the stabilizing adult in this offense.

What I’d put next to it, as context and not as an excuse: a glute injury in Week 2. A cracked rib on Monday. A defense generating a thirty-one percent pressure rate. And three brand-new starters along the offensive line.

Context doesn’t erase eight interceptions. But it does explain some of them.

UPDATE FIVE: Drew Lock — and the part everyone is underselling.

Lock is starting. He was limited Wednesday and limited again Thursday with the same right elbow. Be precise about what that means: two straight limited days is not progress. It’s also not a setback. It’s the status quo, being managed on purpose.

Macdonald: “He’s able to throw. How much, we’re working through that right now.” And: “He’s going to throw the ball throughout the week, but we’re just going to manage it. He’s not going to take 8,000 throws.” And: “It’s a different injury than Sam had in like the playoffs.”

And the origin is perfect Seattle — “we practiced in the rain and he got it... it got aggravated in a rain practice.” Sunday’s starting quarterback hurt his throwing elbow practicing in the rain in the Pacific Northwest in October. You cannot make that up.

Now here’s what nobody is saying loudly enough. Drew Lock has already done this to this exact football team.

Week 2. At Arizona. Nineteen of twenty-six. Two hundred thirty-five yards. Three touchdowns — all three of them to Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Final score thirty-one to seven.

On the season he’s completing seventy-three percent of his passes at 8.8 yards an attempt with four touchdowns and zero interceptions.

Zero.

Small sample, and I’m telling you that up front. But it’s the same opponent, the same receiver and the same quarterback — and the last time those three were in a room together, Arizona lost by twenty-four.

UPDATE SIX: the one that actually worries me — Quenton Nelson.

Nelson was limited on Wednesday with a knee. On Thursday he did not practice at all.

That’s a downgrade, on a short week, for the Pro Bowl guard Seattle gave up a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick to acquire nine days ago.

And it collides with something Macdonald said Wednesday. Asked whether Anthony Bradford’s forty career starts at right guard made him the better option over moving Grey Zabel there: “I think we had to make the decision and go, so we’re committed to that. We’re confident in AB to go in and play good football for us. But also, Grey’s going to continue to get better.”

“We’re committed to that.” So the plan is Nelson at left guard, Zabel at right guard.

But if Nelson can’t go, that plan doesn’t exist. Zabel slides back to the left side, where he’s played his whole career, and Anthony Bradford — limited both Wednesday and Thursday with a knee of his own — starts at right guard.

Twelves, I made an entire video arguing against trading Anthony Bradford — because Seattle had just rebuilt three spots on that line and Bradford was the only guard on the roster with forty starts at right guard. “You do not sell your insurance policy in the same week you create the risk.”

Ten days later, Nelson’s knee kept him off the practice field and Bradford might be starting. That’s not a victory lap. That’s just the reason depth exists.

UPDATE SEVEN: the rest of the report, and it’s a lot.

Seventeen Seahawks were on Thursday’s injury report. Nine of them did not practice.

Did not practice: Charles Cross with the ankle. Rashid Shaheed with the knee. Leonard Williams with the stinger. Quenton Nelson with the knee. Derick Hall with the back. Connor O’Toole with the foot. Brandon Pili with the back. And Sam Darnold.

Limited: Drew Lock, Anthony Bradford, Velus Jones Junior — and Ernest Jones, who is new to the report with an ankle.

Ernest Jones is your defensive signal-caller. Him showing up on an injury report two days before a division game is not a footnote.

Full participation: Nick Emmanwori with the shoulder, Tory Horton with the hand, Tyrice Knight with the thigh. Jaxon Smith-Njigba was down Wednesday as a rest day, not an injury.

Now the caveat, and it’s a real one. This is a six-day week off a Monday night game. Macdonald said Wednesday was effectively a recovery day — “for all intents and purposes, it’s a Wednesday at this point.” On a short week veterans get rested by design, and a “did not practice” on Thursday can mean a hot tub instead of a hamstring.

His Wednesday line still stands: “I’d say they all have a chance to play. We haven’t ruled anybody out yet.”

Friday’s report is the one that decides Sunday. Watch it.

UPDATE EIGHT: and this is the one I find most revealing, because it’s the market’s opinion instead of ours.

This game opened with Seattle favored by eleven and a half points. It got as high as twelve and a half. It is now sitting around eight and a half.

And the total opened at forty-four and a half. It’s now thirty-nine and a half.

Read that again.

The betting market repriced this football game by four points of spread and five points of total — on the quarterback news alone.

That is the clearest outside measurement you will ever get of what this week cost Seattle. The market is saying, in dollars, that Drew Lock instead of Sam Darnold is worth roughly three to four points, and that the injuries on both sides are worth five points of scoring coming off the board.

My own read: the market overcorrected on the quarterback and undercorrected on how broken Arizona is. More on that shortly.

UPDATE NINE: John Schneider spoke, and the deadline is Tuesday.

On the Seattle Radio Network, the general manager said this: “We talk about being able to improve your team all throughout the year and keeping your powder dry a little bit.” He added: “We’re still talking to several teams.” And on timing: “The majority of the teams will wait until the very deadline.”

One honesty note: he said that before Monday night’s game. So that’s a temperature read from before the loss, not after it. Treat it accordingly.

But “keeping your powder dry” is worth sitting with from a man who just traded a 2027 third and a 2028 fifth for a Pro Bowl guard. He already fired one shot. He’s telling you he has more. And the deadline is Tuesday, November tenth.

Now the bigger question somebody raised this week, and I want to answer it properly — and label it properly.

The question is: if this season goes sideways because of the quarterback room, does Seattle reset it? Let Darnold hit free agency, hand the job to Jalen Milroe on a cheap rookie deal, and use the savings on the offensive line and the backfield?

First, the label: that is speculation, not reporting. Nobody in that building has said one word about it, and be suspicious of anyone who tells you otherwise.

Second, the structural argument is real. A quarterback on a rookie contract is the cheapest competitive advantage in football. It’s how you afford a real left guard and a real running back at the same time.

Third — here’s where I come down: I’m against it, and not for sentimental reasons. Jalen Milroe has never thrown a pass in an NFL game. Not one. You do not hand a season to a quarterback with zero career attempts because eight interceptions made you impatient. That’s not a plan, that’s a flinch.

And the thing I keep coming back to: a man who played four quarters on a cracked rib and never told his own head coach is not what’s wrong with this football team. Fix the pass protection and the slow starts first.

Now let me turn the camera around, because Arizona is in worse shape than we are and almost nobody is saying it.

Running back Jeremiyah Love is out four to five weeks with a high ankle sprain. Bam Knight is in concussion protocol. That leaves Tyler Allgeier and Evan Hull carrying a backfield.

Left tackle Paris Johnson Junior is out for the season. Wide receivers Kendrick Bourne and Jalen Brooks are out for the season. That makes Simi Fehoko a starter next to Michael Wilson — and Michael Wilson is limited in practice because he broke two ribs.

Think about that symmetry. Seattle’s quarterback cracked a rib and played through it. Arizona’s number one receiver broke two of them and is trying to. This is a brutal sport.

And Arizona’s line also has Jayden Williams and Jon Gaines the Second banged up on top of losing Johnson for the year.

And at quarterback: Gardner Minshew the Second is starting, with rookie Carson Beck as the backup — which means Jacoby Brissett has been demoted to third.

Credit where it’s due. Minshew came off the bench at halftime against Dallas and went seventeen of twenty-three for 188 yards and three touchdowns to win in overtime, from fourteen points down — against franchise history that said Arizona was one and one hundred forty-seven and one since the year 2000 when trailing by fourteen or more in the fourth quarter.

That’s real. But it was half a game, at home. Sunday is a road start at Lumen Field.

And one last number, which should settle your stomach: Seattle has won the last ten straight meetings with Arizona. Ten and oh.
