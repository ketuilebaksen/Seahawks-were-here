"Drew's going to start here for a while."

Seven words. That's what Mike Macdonald said this week when he was asked about the quarterback situation, and Seahawks Nation, I don't think enough people stopped to think about what that sentence actually means. He didn't say "Drew's starting this week." He didn't say "we'll see how it goes." He said "for a while." And then he said something else — he said the team is rolling with two quarterbacks, Drew Lock and Jalen Milroe, with no plans to go get anybody. No emergency signing. No veteran off the street. Seattle looked at the situation and decided the answer is already in the building.

Today, in Arizona, Drew Lock gets his first start of the 2026 season. And this is the most confident I have heard this organization sound about a backup quarterback in a very long time.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. It's game day, and before kickoff I want to walk you through everything Mike Macdonald said this week, the report that set this fan base on fire, and exactly what Drew Lock has to do to keep the streak alive.

Let's start with the official word, because for a few days there was a lot of noise.

Macdonald laid it out plainly: "Sam's going to be out this game. Drew will start for us." Then the key part: they don't anticipate putting Darnold on injured reserve. They'll reevaluate every day, reevaluate next week, and go from there. And on Darnold's rehab, Macdonald said he's "killing it." He's doing a lot. He just can't play this week.

Notice what Seattle didn't do. There was some talk that maybe the Seahawks would keep Arizona guessing — list Darnold as questionable, make the Cardinals prepare for two different quarterbacks and dilute their game plan. Seattle didn't bother with that. They ruled him out early and named Lock the starter. That's a team with nothing to hide and no interest in games. It's Drew Lock. Prepare for him.

And then the question every fan was asking: are you comfortable with only two quarterbacks, or are you going to add somebody? Macdonald's answer: "We're going to roll with Drew and Jalen. He'll get all the reps. He's our starter this week, and we'll operate accordingly." And then — "Drew's going to start here for a while."

All the reps. That's the part that matters for today. Remember, last Wednesday Lock came off the bench having taken basically no first-team reps. Now he's had a full week — eleven days, actually, because the opener was on a Wednesday — of every single rep with the starters. With Jaxon Smith-Njigba. With Cooper Kupp. With the tight ends. And now with Tory Horton, who's back from his hamstring.

Now let me read you what Macdonald said about Drew Lock as a person and a player, because I think it's the best thing a head coach has said about a backup quarterback in years.

"Drew's a proven, really good player in this league. When he started, he's played really good football. And since he's been on our team, I've seen him play in person. He's a heck of a player here. Heck of a guy, heck of a teammate."

And then he described exactly why Lock fits what Seattle does. He said Lock has a great balance about him — a true professional, but also loose and focused. He called him "a warrior in the garden" — which is that idea of being the calmest, most peaceful guy in the room who can flip into a fighter the second it's needed. And he said: you attack all the things, you earn the opportunity to play, and then when you go out there, you go rip it.

Macdonald was also asked if he'd noticed anything different about Lock this week — a spring in his step, extra enthusiasm now that he's the starter. And his answer was great: no. "He's Drew. That's the way Drew is every day." A joyful guy. His own personality, which they love. And his style of playing quarterback is what Seattle wants — within the scheme, but being himself.

Think about that. The head coach of the defending champions is saying his backup's personality doesn't change when he becomes the starter because he's always carried himself like one.

Now, Macdonald also made sure nobody got carried away, and I respect this. He said Lock should be proud of himself — but it was one week. The message to the whole team right now is urgency: stack plays, stack practices, stack performances. Take everything to another level. That's a coach who knows one great night doesn't make a season.

And when Macdonald calls Lock "proven," he's not exaggerating. As a rookie in Denver, Lock went 4-1 in his five starts and tied John Elway for the most wins by a rookie quarterback in Broncos history. In December of 2023, right here in Seattle, he took over down 17-13 to Philadelphia with 1:52 left, drove 92 yards, and hit Jaxon Smith-Njigba for a 29-yard touchdown with twenty-eight seconds on the clock. And last Wednesday, he did it again — a 45-yard touchdown to the same receiver, on fourth and one, to tie the game against New England, and then a run to the three-yard line that set up the winning field goal. 16 of 22, 187 yards, zero turnovers, 113.3 rating. Twice now, with the building on fire, he's found JSN.

And there's one more piece of the quarterback room worth mentioning today. With no third quarterback being added, Jalen Milroe is the backup. And back in camp, Macdonald said something about Milroe that reads very differently now: "We're gonna have plays for Jalen in game plans, and he's gonna rep those with the ones." Milroe ran a 4.40 forty and scored twenty rushing touchdowns at Alabama. So don't be shocked if you see a package or two for him today — especially near the goal line. That's not a sign Seattle doesn't trust Lock. It's a sign they're using every weapon they've got.

Now let's talk about the report that set this fan base off, because the reporter behind it responded this week and I think it's worth hearing.

Mike Florio reported last weekend that Darnold could miss four to six weeks. And a lot of Seahawks fans — and some local media, according to him — were furious about it. His response was pretty blunt. He said if he was going to make something up, he'd come up with something far more interesting. That's what he was told by someone in a position to know. And then he stressed the language: could miss four to six weeks. Not will miss. Could.

And honestly? He's right about the wording, and I think this is a good lesson for all of us. "Could" is a range, not a prediction. Since that report, ESPN's Jeremy Fowler said Darnold will miss this week and possibly Week 3. Mike Garafolo reported roughly a four-week return. And Seattle says it doesn't expect to use injured reserve. All of those can be true at the same time. The realistic window is somewhere between two and five weeks, and the team will know more every day.

Then Week 5, the 49ers at home. And that's the game you'd most love to have Sam Darnold back for. Remember, San Francisco beat Seattle at Lumen Field to open last season, 17-13, in Darnold's very first game as a Seahawk. That one still stings.

Then at Denver in prime time. Then Kansas City comes to town in Week 7 — with Kenneth Walker, last year's Super Bowl MVP, now wearing a Chiefs uniform after running for 173 yards in his debut. Then Chicago.

And here's what makes today bigger than just one division game. Seattle has won eleven straight, counting the playoffs, which ties the longest streak in franchise history. A win today makes it twelve. A new franchise record. With the backup quarterback. On the road.

Think about the teams that have held that record before in this franchise. The Legion of Boom era, the Russell Wilson years, the Beast Mode teams — none of them ever won twelve in a row. If Drew Lock walks out of Arizona with a win today, his name goes on something that Seattle's greatest teams never touched. For a guy who was a throw-in in a trade four years ago, that's a pretty incredible sentence.

Now let me tell you what I'm watching for this afternoon, because I want you watching the same things.

Number one: Drew Lock's first quarter. Last week he came in cold. Today he has a game plan built around him. If he's crisp early — clean operation, no double-checking at the line, getting the ball out on time — that tells you the extra week of reps made a real difference.

Number two: turnovers. That's the whole assignment. Lock was perfect with the football against New England. The Cardinals' defense will try to make him hold it and force something. If he protects it again, Seattle's defense wins this game.

Number three: the run game. Rookie Jadarian Price flashed last week, and every analyst expects Seattle to lean on him today. A backup quarterback with a strong running game behind him is a completely different quarterback.

Number four: Trey McBride. Seattle is thin at safety with Ty Okada out and Nick Emmanwori questionable, and McBride is one of the best tight ends in the league. That's the matchup that could keep Arizona in this game.

And number five: the new weapon. Tory Horton scored five touchdowns on thirteen catches as a rookie last season before his leg gave out. He's cleared today. If Lock finds him early, that's a whole new dimension Arizona didn't have to prepare for last week.

Arizona has problems of its own, too. Cornerback Garrett Williams is out with an Achilles injury, and they came into the weekend with starting guards Isaac Seumalo and Isaiah Adams both questionable. So both teams are banged up.
