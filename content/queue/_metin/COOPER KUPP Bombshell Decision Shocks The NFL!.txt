Okay twelves, let me translate "MATCHY," because this is the most important coverage concept in modern football and most people have never had it explained.

Everybody knows the two basic ideas. MAN coverage: you follow one guy everywhere. ZONE coverage: you guard an area and whoever enters it.

MATCH coverage — "matchy" — is the hybrid, and it's what almost every good defense actually plays now.

It starts as a zone. The defender reads the route distribution in front of him. And then based on RULES, he MATCHES up man-to-man on a specific receiver and carries him.

So it LOOKS like zone before the snap. And it PLAYS like man after it.

And that's why Kupp's point is so sharp: "you aren't getting a TRUE MAN call — but they play MATCHY defense." Meaning the thirty percent man number undersells it. Even San Francisco's zone snaps were functionally man coverage once the routes declared.

And "CARRY PLAYERS in their single-high stuff" means exactly that — a defender whose job is to pick up a receiver and CARRY him down the field instead of passing him off.

And "QUARTER stuff" is quarters coverage — four deep defenders splitting the field — where the corners and safeties match receivers based on what the route combination does.

Twelves, this is the hardest thing in football for a quarterback and a receiver to solve, because the picture you see before the snap is not the coverage you get after it.

And Kupp's job description for the week is perfect: "whatever their plan was, whatever their reason for doing it — OUR JOB IS TO MAKE SURE WE HAVE PLANS FOR AS MANY OF THOSE CONTINGENCIES AS POSSIBLE, THAT ALLOWS US TO STILL PLAY FAST."

"THAT ALLOWS US TO STILL PLAY FAST."

Twelves, that's the whole trick, and it's the trap of over-preparing.

You could install an answer for every single thing San Francisco might do. And then your quarterback is thinking so hard about which answer applies that he's late on every throw.
