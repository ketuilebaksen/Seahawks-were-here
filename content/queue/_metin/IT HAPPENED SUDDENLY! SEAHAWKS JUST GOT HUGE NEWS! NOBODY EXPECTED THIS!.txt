Jadarian Price was limited on Wednesday, and George Holani was limited too with a knee issue from the Arizona game. Ty Okada is still out with the hamstring, and Brandon Pili did not practice as he works through the concussion protocol.

And the Price news this week was the one that made me exhale. Remember, when he left that game it was reported as a chest injury, and he'd already had X-rays. Macdonald cleared it up: it's actually a shoulder issue, and the shoulder is intact. Nothing serious, no expectation of a long absence.

That matters a lot, because Price has been exactly what Seattle hoped for. Through roughly his first seven quarters as a pro he's been averaging north of four and a half yards a carry. He hasn't scored yet — that's coming — but the explosiveness has been obvious from his very first snaps.

And here's the part I want to highlight about Arizona specifically, because it explains a lot about that game. The Cardinals came out aggressively trying to stop the run. They sold out to it early. That's what made the ground game harder in the first half — and it's exactly what opened up the passing game. That 82-yard touchdown to Jaxon Smith-Njigba didn't happen in a vacuum. It happened because Arizona was so worried about the run that Budda Baker got aggressive and JSN ran right past everybody.

And even with all that attention, Price still produced. That's the thing about him: he's not the biggest back on the field, but his elusiveness makes up for it, and his vision is what stands out most. He sees where the lane is developing before it's actually there, and then he explodes through it.

What's more, this backfield isn't dependent on him. Emanuel Wilson ran it well — 21 carries, 91 yards, including ten carries for 48 yards on that ten-minute closing drive. George Holani has contributed too. Zach Charbonnet is still out, but Price, Wilson and Holani give Fleury three real options. Seattle actually came close to its first individual 100-yard rushing game of the season in Arizona.

And one more note on the Price situation that's worth understanding. Seattle drafted him 32nd overall — a first-round pick on a running back, which this league doesn't do lightly anymore. They let Kenneth Walker, the Super Bowl MVP, walk to Kansas City and handed this job to a rookie. So every time Price touches the football, there's a little extra weight on it. Two games in, he's averaging over four and a half a carry with one fumble he'll learn from. That's a fine start, and the shoulder news means we get to keep watching it.

Which brings me to Brian Fleury, and I want to make his case properly.

Put yourself in his shoes. He walks into 2026 as a first-time play-caller in the National Football League. The biggest game of his professional life, the season opener, in front of the whole country, defending a Super Bowl. And five plays in, his quarterback is on the ground.

Everything he had prepared — every install, every read, every sequence built around Sam Darnold — had to change in real time, mid-game, with a backup who hadn't taken first-team reps. Then he had to go into Week 2 knowing he'd be doing it again with Lock.

And look at what's happened. Week 1 was survival — thirteen points, one enormous fourth-down throw, and the defense winning the game. Week 2 was a different animal entirely. And the improvement from game one to game two was obvious to anybody watching.

Here's what impressed me most. It wasn't just the explosive play. Yes, the 82-yarder to JSN is the highlight. But an offense living purely on explosives is a fragile offense — one bad week and it scores ten points. What Seattle showed in Arizona was the other thing: they sustained drives. They moved the football. They generated first downs. They attacked in different ways. And then in the fourth quarter they put together sixteen plays, seventy yards, ten minutes of clock.

That's a complete offensive profile: hit the home run, grind out the long drive, and run it when everybody knows you're running it.

Now, the defense. Because if there's one area where Seattle has made an overwhelming statement, this is it.

Think about the assignment this unit has been handed. Support an offense dealing with a quarterback injury. That's it. Just hold the rope. And they've done more than that — they've dictated both football games.

Against New England, the offense needed turnovers, and the defense produced them: three interceptions on New England's final three possessions. Against Arizona, they simply suffocated the Cardinals in the second half. Arizona managed only four first downs after halftime. One came on a penalty. Another came on the final play of the game.
