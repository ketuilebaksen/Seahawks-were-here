Now here's the part I actually want you to remember from this video, because it's the real stake.

Brian Fleury's passing game is built on play action.

Watch the tape. The big plays this season — the 82-yard bomb to JSN in Arizona, the chunk throws in Washington — come off run fakes. Darnold turns his back, sells the handoff, the linebackers take a half step downhill, and the seam opens up behind them.

A play-action offense is a loan taken out against your run game.

And you can only keep borrowing as long as the defense believes you. Right now defensive coordinators around this league are watching that Washington film, and what they see is a team that could not get twenty yards from a single running back. So what do they do next?

They stop biting on the fake. They stop dropping the linebacker downhill. They keep the safety deep on Jaxon Smith-Njigba, who leads the league in receiving yards and already has six touchdowns. They put an extra man in the pass rush because there's no reason to respect the ground. And suddenly the offense that just put up 437 yards is throwing into a bracketed receiver with a defensive end in the quarterback's face.

That's the whole danger. Not Sunday's loss. The next five Sundays.

The play-action magic doesn't die dramatically. It just stops working one week, and nobody can explain why, and the answer was in a box score three weeks earlier that said forty-four yards.

Fix the run game and this offense is dangerous all year. Don't, and the passing game goes with it.
