Let me give you the full background, because the rules here matter and most people get them wrong.

Charbonnet tore his ACL and had surgery in February. February. That's eight months ago. Because he was still unable to go when rosters were cut at the end of August, Seattle placed him on reserve slash physically-unable-to-perform — and that designation carries a hard rule: he is required to miss the first four games of the season. Not "probably." Required.

Then, starting in Week 5 of the league calendar, the team gets a twenty-one day window. They can open that window, let him practice, and watch him. At the end of those twenty-one days they have to make a decision: activate him to the fifty-three man roster, or shut him down for the entire season. There is no third option and there is no extension.

Seattle opened that window on October 1st.

So here's where it stands. He practiced Thursday as a limited participant. He is not eligible to play against the Chargers on Sunday — that's the four-game rule, and it's absolute. The earliest he can play a down of football is next week.

Next week is San Francisco at Lumen Field.

Now let me tell you why Charbonnet specifically is the right answer to this specific problem, because this isn't just "a body returns."

Look at what Seattle's backfield actually is right now. Jadarian Price is the explosive one — the first-round pick, the home-run threat, the guy who finds a crease and takes it fifty. Fourth and goal from the two. Seattle went for it. The pass fell incomplete. They lost by two points.

That is a Zach Charbonnet play. That's the exact down and distance he exists for. You don't throw it there if you've got a guy you trust to get twenty-four inches, and Seattle didn't have that guy on the roster.
