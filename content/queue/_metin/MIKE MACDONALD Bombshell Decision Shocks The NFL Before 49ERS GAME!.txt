“There are not going to be big group meetings.”

Seahawks Nation, that’s Mike Macdonald describing how Seattle is spending this bye week — and it is NOT what most of you expected.

He went on his radio show and DOWNPLAYED how much extra work this team would do. No practice grind. No marathon film sessions.

His words, in spirit: get away from the game a little. Let yourself reset. FALL BACK IN LOVE WITH IT — while still making sure you’re preparing.

And instead of practicing, Seattle is doing something specific: a SELF-SCOUTING WEEK.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Seattle is six and four. San Francisco is nine days out. And twelves, I want to walk you through how beaten up this team actually is, what self-scouting means, and why the Niners’ next opponent might be the best news Seattle gets all month. Let’s go.

First — the injury list, and twelves, it’s worse than I think people realize.

EIGHT KEY PLAYERS either dealing with an injury right now or just coming out of one.

Let me run it. Multiple injuries across the offensive line. BOTH quarterbacks dealing with injury issues. George Holani now week to week with a sprained MCL. Jarran Reed missing time in the last game. And Leonard Williams, who hasn’t played in TWO WEEKS.

And the honest caveat that I appreciated: “those are only the names I am SURE ABOUT. I am not even counting the players who are FIGHTING THROUGH THINGS WE HAVE NEVER HEARD ABOUT, or who missed a couple of plays and it was brushed off with a shrug because he is a special teams guy.”

And on that point — a detail I did not know and it changes how you see one of these injuries:

“GEORGE HOLANI is on EVERY SPECIAL TEAMS UNIT THE SEAHAWKS HAVE. When a player like that is out, IT MATTERS.”

Every unit. Twelves, that’s a man whose absence shows up in four different places on a Sunday and never once in a box score.

And we saw the cost directly. Mike Macdonald after the Raiders game: “with GEORGE HOLANI GOING DOWN, we had to take JD off of kickoff return. So those are guys that HAVEN’T NECESSARILY PRACTICED A BUNCH IN A WHILE.”

Holani going down is what pulled Jadarian Price off kickoff return — and what put Tory Horton in position to leap ten feet in the air to keep that kickoff alive. One special teams injury rewrote the end of a football game.

And on Drew Lock, this was fair: “even in the case of Drew Lock, where I think it was more of an ELBOW TENDINITIS thing and he is FULLY THROUGH IT — he still belongs on the list.”

He does. Elbow tendinitis plus a strained oblique, and he won two football games throwing on exactly ONE DAY of each practice week.

NOW — the comparison to last season, and twelves, this is the part that actually explains this whole year.

“LAST YEAR, there were stretches when a player or two went down, but it tended to be SHORT AND SHARP. Someone had a concussion and missed a game. Someone had an oblique problem and missed a game. DIFFERENT PLAYERS, DIFFERENT WEEKS, BACK QUICKLY.”

“THIS YEAR, guys have been out for MULTIPLE GAMES AT A TIME.”

And the kicker: “it took until basically OCTOBER for the Seahawks defense to be ONE HUNDRED PERCENT HEALTHY — and now it is the OFFENSE dealing with the problem. I AM NOT SURE WE HAVE SEEN THE ENTIRE OFFENSIVE GROUP HEALTHY AT THE SAME TIME ALL YEAR.”

Not once. Not one single week.

And it lines up perfectly with what Mike Macdonald said about his quarterback this week — that he can’t wait for Sam Darnold to be fully healthy “MAYBE FOR THE FIRST TIME THIS YEAR.”

Twelves, think about that as a whole picture. Seattle is six and four and has never once fielded a healthy offense.

AND NOW the explanation of WHY that’s so much worse than it sounds, and this is genuinely insightful:

“When a starter is out, the player behind him steps in — and then THE PLAYER BEHIND THAT PLAYER MOVES UP, and the WHOLE DEPTH CHART SHIFTS. Coaches have to adjust the game plan to what the AVAILABLE players can do. Players who are on the field together FOR THE FIRST TIME have to LEARN EACH OTHER’S HABITS ON THE FLY.”

And the conclusion: “over a few games, that SHUFFLING CAN WEAR ON A TEAM in a way that a SINGLE INJURY NEVER WOULD.”

Twelves, that’s the hidden cost and nobody talks about it.

One man going out costs you one man. But a man going out for FOUR games means his backup plays, the third-stringer becomes the backup, somebody comes off the practice squad to be the third — and now you’ve got four relationships being built from scratch while trying to win games.

And it hits the offensive line hardest, which is exactly where Seattle has been hit. A national analyst nailed this: that group is “built on CHEMISTRY AND TRUST, where everyone has to do their job SOMETIMES WITHOUT ASKING.”

Count Seattle’s line configurations in a month. They traded for Quenton Nelson and moved Grey Zabel to right guard. Nelson needed knee surgery. Zabel went back to the left and Anthony Bradford came back to the right. Charles Cross out the whole time. Then Abe Lucas hurt his ankle. Bobby Hart playing meaningful snaps.

And Mike Macdonald’s own words: “with the MOVEMENT that we’ve had across our line in the last two weeks, to kind of COME TOGETHER — and especially FINISH THE GAME the way they did — was really cool.”

That’s what the shuffle costs. And it’s why “getting everybody back in one room” is worth more than any trade.

NOW — the decision, and twelves, this is the real headline.

Mike Macdonald made it plain on his radio show: “this is NOT NECESSARILY A SITUATION WHERE MORE PRACTICE TIME IS GOING TO HAPPEN ON THE FIELD. He even DOWNPLAYED how much extra work the team would be doing. It is more of a SELF-SCOUTING WEEK. THERE ARE NOT GOING TO BE BIG GROUP MEETINGS.”

And the philosophy: “it is about GETTING AWAY FROM THE GAME A LITTLE, LETTING YOURSELF RESET AND FALLING BACK IN LOVE WITH IT — while also making sure that you are PREPARING.”

“Falling back in love with it.”

Twelves, I want to tell you why I think that’s the correct call and not soft.

Because the instinct — especially from a fanbase — is to say: we’re six and four, we’re outside the playoff picture, we’ve got San Francisco next. GRIND. Put the pads on. Practice twice a day.

And that would be the single worst thing Mike Macdonald could do.

Because the problem is not effort. The problem is BODIES. This team has eight key players hurt or just coming off injury, and has never once had a healthy offense. Practicing them harder doesn’t make them healthier. It makes them more hurt.

And the honest framing on the mental side was perfect: “a team that has been dealing with injuries week after week is GRINDING IN A WAY THAT DOES NOT SHOW UP IN THE STANDINGS. A mental reset is NOT A SIGN OF SOFTNESS. It is a recognition that PEOPLE ARE PEOPLE.”

And the key point: “Macdonald is NOT ASKING FOR A VACATION. His message was about BALANCE. Get away from the game enough to reset your mind, but DO NOT LOSE THE THREAD. That is a HARD LINE TO WALK, because TOO MUCH REST CAN LEAVE A TEAM FLAT and TOO LITTLE CAN LEAVE IT FRIED.”

Twelves, that’s exactly the needle, and I trust this coach to thread it — because we have watched him thread it all season.

This is the same man who after a seventeen-point home loss said: “as a coach you get emotional. HOWEVER, you do have to identify the positive things that do happen. IT’S NOT ALL BAD.” And who after a seventeen to three win refused to oversell it: “I thought we took a step today.”

He doesn’t overreact in either direction. That’s the whole reason to believe him here.

NOW — let me explain SELF-SCOUTING, because this is the actual work happening this week.

“It means a team STUDIES ITSELF THE WAY AN OPPONENT WOULD. You look at your own film and ask WHAT TENDENCIES YOU HAVE, WHERE YOUR WEAKNESSES ARE, and WHAT OTHER TEAMS ARE DOING TO EXPLOIT THEM. Then you try to turn those weaknesses into strengths.”

Twelves, this is enormous, and I want to connect it to something Mike Macdonald said weeks ago that I’ve never forgotten.

Remember his answer about first-down passing? He said: “you have to MINIMIZE THE AMOUNT OF TIMES EVERYBODY KNOWS YOU’RE DROPPING BACK. Sometimes you need to call pass-run-run-pass. Because THERE’S PSYCHOS OUT THERE THAT HAVE COMPUTERS that find out WHEN you’re going to pass the ball and HOW you’re going to pass it. And now you just gave them the advantage.”

THAT is what self-scouting defends against.

Every team in this league has an analytics department building tendency reports on Seattle right now. They know Seattle’s pass rate on first and ten from the shotgun with eleven personnel. They know it to the decimal.

So self-scouting is Seattle hiring the psychos with the computers to look at THEMSELVES. Find your own patterns before the Niners do. Then break them.

And the mechanism for why it only happens now: “a NORMAL WEEK has a schedule that DOES NOT LEAVE MUCH ROOM for anything but the next opponent. Walkthroughs, meetings, and film are all pointed at ONE TARGET, and there is NO TIME LEFT TO LOOK INWARD. A BYE WEEK BREAKS THAT PATTERN. It lets a staff ask the BIGGER QUESTIONS — like WHY a certain play has not worked, or WHY an opponent has had success against a certain look.”

And the payoff: “teams that answer those questions in the middle of the season usually come out of it with a CLEARER IDENTITY for the final stretch.”

“A clearer identity.”

Twelves, that word should ring a bell. Julian Love told us after the Bears loss: “I think we’re still in IDENTITY-FINDING MODE. Right now, IT DOES NOT FEEL HOW IT’S SUPPOSED TO LOOK.”

And Macdonald after the Arizona win: “I don’t care about the score. I just want us to GO BE US AND FEEL LIKE US. And A LOT OF TIMES WE FELT THAT WAY.”

This bye week is where that identity gets finished. And Macdonald has a list — he named it himself after the Raiders game: “there’s some things we definitely need to CLEAN UP. The TAKEAWAYS, FINISHING THE RED ZONE, things like that — really on BOTH SIDES.”

Takeaways. Red zone. Both sides of the ball. That’s the self-scout.

And the smart prediction about how the week actually goes: “I would also bet the work does not stop there. EARLY IN THE WEEK, you are looking at YOURSELF. BY THE END OF THE WEEK, you are already looking at SAN FRANCISCO.”

That’s right. Self-scout Monday through Wednesday, then flip to the Niners. Fix yourself, then go find them.

AND NOW — the news that might matter more than anything Seattle does this week.

THE 49ERS PLAY THE MINNESOTA VIKINGS ON SUNDAY NIGHT.

And twelves, here’s why that’s enormous:

“Minnesota is a MUCH BETTER TEAM NOW than it was even three weeks ago. The Vikings made a move at the trade deadline to get KIRK COUSINS because they knew they had a chance to be a CHAMPIONSHIP TEAM and did not want to miss it for lack of a quarterback. He may not be an ABOVE AVERAGE quarterback anymore, but he is certainly an AVERAGE one — and Minnesota is using him to the HIGHEST LEVEL OF HIS ABILITY.”

And twelves, remember where Kirk Cousins came from: he was the Raiders’ quarterback. Las Vegas traded him to Minnesota and handed the job to a rookie — and we watched what that produced in Week 10. Six sacks and twenty-seven total yards in a half.

Meanwhile Cousins beat the Packers for his new team on that same Sunday.

And now here’s the part I love:

“Plenty of people say the TWO BEST DEFENSES IN THE NFL are MINNESOTA with Brian Flores and SEATTLE with Mike Macdonald.”

“What matters is that the 49ers have to go BACK-TO-BACK WEEKS against what many consider the TWO BEST DEFENSES IN THE LEAGUE. I DO NOT SEE HOW THEY COME OUT OF THAT TWO AND OH. I think there is a BETTER CHANCE THEY GO OH AND TWO.”

Twelves, sit with that schedule.

San Francisco gets Brian Flores on Sunday night — a man whose entire reputation is built on exotic, overwhelming pressure packages. And then, six days later, they get Mike Macdonald — a man whose defense just allowed twenty-seven yards in a half and held Kansas City to three points.

Two of the most creative defensive minds in football, consecutively.

And the physical point, which is real and he was careful about it: “I am NOT ROOTING FOR INJURIES. I am saying that A PHYSICAL GAME TAKES SOMETHING OUT OF A TEAM, and that matters for THE TEAM THAT COMES NEXT.”

Good. That’s the right way to say it.

And twelves, it’s just true. A Sunday night war against a Brian Flores defense leaves bruises, and bruises don’t heal in six days. Seattle will have had FOURTEEN days of rest. San Francisco will have had six days and a brawl.

That’s an enormous edge, and Seattle didn’t have to do anything to earn it.

And the honest hedge: “YES, it is a home game for the 49ers. YES, they SHOULD win it. But SHOULD WIN AND WILL WIN ARE NOT THE SAME THING.”

NOW — who to root for on Sunday night, because there’s a real debate here.

“Some of you will say it is BETTER FOR THE WILD CARD RACE IF THE VIKINGS LOSE. And I get where you are coming from. SEATTLE COULD NOT PASS MINNESOTA with a Vikings loss. But MORE FANS are saying they want the 49ers to falter, and THAT IS WHERE I AM SITTING TOO. I am PULLING FOR THE VIKINGS.”

And twelves, I’m with him, and here’s my reasoning.

Seattle plays San Francisco. Seattle does NOT play Minnesota.

That’s the whole argument. You always want the teams you still have to face to be as beat up and as beatable as possible. Minnesota’s record doesn’t affect anything Seattle can control — but San Francisco limping into Week 12 absolutely does.

And the division math is enormous. San Francisco is EIGHT AND ONE. Seattle is six and four. If the Niners lose Sunday night and then lose to Seattle, that’s eight and three versus seven and four, with Seattle holding the head-to-head.

Suddenly the NFC West is a real race again.

NOW — the playoff picture, and twelves, here’s the uncomfortable truth.

“RIGHT NOW, EVERY SINGLE TEAM IN THE NORTH IS IN.”

Every one. Detroit, Green Bay, Minnesota, Chicago.

And Seattle and the Rams are tied at six and four, both on the outside.

But here’s the structural hope, and it’s just arithmetic: “I believe a couple of those teams WILL FALTER, BECAUSE THEY ALL STILL HAVE TO PLAY EACH OTHER.”

Twelves, that’s not optimism. That’s math. Every NFC North game produces a loser, and all four of those teams are in Seattle’s way.

Plus the injuries are mounting around them. Detroit lost Aidan Hutchinson for the YEAR with a significant knee injury. Minnesota sent a receiver to injured reserve.

And the honest version of Seattle’s position: “these teams are going to BEAT EACH OTHER UP, and Seattle is going to NEED A LITTLE BIT OF HELP. If EVERYONE IN FRONT OF SEATTLE ENDS UP WINNING OUT, that is a problem. THE GOOD NEWS IS THAT TEAMS DO NOT WIN OUT.”

Teams don’t win out. That’s the whole thing.

And the tiebreaker note, which matters: “a team that does not win its division has to qualify as a WILD CARD. And that route runs through a HEAD-TO-HEAD TIEBREAKER PILE that can get MESSY when several clubs finish with the same record. A LOSS BY A CONFERENCE RIVAL CAN MATTER NEARLY AS MUCH AS A WIN OF YOUR OWN.”

NOW — the remaining schedule, and twelves, I’m going to give you his read and then my honest caveats.

His assessment: “the 49ers game is going to be AN ABSOLUTE WAR. But the DALLAS COWBOYS ARE NOT A GOOD TEAM. The NEW YORK GIANTS ARE SUSCEPTIBLE TO LOSING GAMES. The PHILADELPHIA EAGLES ARE NOT A GOOD TEAM EITHER. The RAMS ARE NOT AS GOOD AS WE THOUGHT. After the Rams game you get the PANTHERS — not a very good team — and then THE RAMS ONE MORE TIME.”

And he labeled it himself, which I respect: “THESE ARE MY JUDGMENTS, and I know a fan will disagree with any of them.”

Twelves, here’s my honest take on that list.

I think he’s right about the SHAPE of it and a little aggressive on the specifics.

Calling the Eagles “not a good team” is a big swing. That’s a franchise that’s been among the best in the NFC. And Dallas is at home on MONDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL — primetime at Lumen Field cuts both ways, but a nationally televised game against the Cowboys is never a gimme.

But the overall point stands and it matters: after San Francisco, Seattle does not play a single team that’s currently being talked about as elite. Three NFC East opponents, Carolina, and the Rams twice.

And the caveat he gave is the one I’d give too: “SCHEDULES LOOK SOFT ON PAPER RIGHT UP UNTIL THE MOMENT A TEAM SHOWS UP FLAT. And every one of those opponents will get a chance to prove me wrong.”

That’s exactly right. Seattle lost at Denver by three. Lost at Washington by two. Lost to Chicago by seventeen at home. This team has absolutely shown it can lose games it shouldn’t.

But the combination is what’s encouraging: “when you add a HEALTHIER ROSTER, a coaching staff that has had A WEEK TO STUDY ITSELF, and a schedule that is NOT PACKED WITH ELITE OPPONENTS — you get a team that has a REAL CHANCE TO CONTROL ITS OWN PATH.”

AND NOW the number he left us with, and twelves, I have to be careful here.

“Since Mike Macdonald became head coach, the Seahawks have done a great job month after month of finding a way to FIX THEIR ISSUES, EXPLOIT THE WEAKNESSES of their opponents, and WIN FOOTBALL GAMES. In that time frame, the Seahawks under Macdonald were SIXTEEN AND TWO.”

Sixteen and two.

And he credited it to a social media post, which is honest of him — and twelves, I’m going to be honest right back: I’m not certain what split that number describes.

It’s not his overall record. So it’s some specific category — possibly after a loss, possibly off extended rest, possibly in a particular month.

So I’m telling you it’s circulating, I’m telling you I can’t verify what it measures, and I’m not going to build an argument on a number I can’t define. That’s the deal on this channel.

But here’s the claim underneath it that I CAN verify, and it’s the one that matters: this staff fixes things between games.

Look at the evidence from the last three weeks alone. Julian Love said the red zone was “TERRIBLE.” Six days later Seattle got its first red zone stops of the season. Macdonald said the offensive line needed to go back where it “banked all those reps” — they did, and allowed zero sacks. He said Dante Fowler’s skill set hadn’t shown “BECAUSE OF THE TYPE OF GAMES WE’VE BEEN IN” — then the defense created passing situations and Fowler got sacks in two straight games.

Name a problem, fix it the following week. That’s this staff’s actual track record, and you don’t need a stat from social media to see it.

AND LAST — the precedent, and twelves, this one gave me chills.

“WE SAW IT LAST YEAR, TOO. The Seahawks went on A HISTORIC RUN and DID NOT LOSE A GAME AFTER THAT FIRST MEETING WITH THE RAMS.”

Did not lose a game. And that run ended in confetti.

And the honest close: “I DO NOT KNOW THAT THIS TEAM CAN DO THAT AGAIN, but it certainly feels like IF ANY TEAM WERE GOING TO, IT WOULD BE THIS ONE.”

Let me bring it home.

Here’s your bye week, twelves.

Eight key players hurt or coming off injury, on a team that has never once had a healthy offense all season. George Holani on every special teams unit, out week to week with a sprained MCL. Leonard Williams two weeks without playing. Nobody expected to go on injured reserve. Quenton Nelson with nine days left and a first practice to watch for. Charles Cross targeting the 49ers game.
