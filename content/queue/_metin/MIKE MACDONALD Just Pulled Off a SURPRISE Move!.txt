It’s game day, twelves — and the real surprise move is the one Seattle has quietly decided NOT to make.

The Seattle Seahawks are off the Jonathan Taylor list.

Adam Schefter’s latest reporting on which teams have inquired about the Colts running back names Denver, Green Bay, Minnesota and the New York Giants.

Seattle isn’t on it.

And here’s why — and twelves, this is the vindication of everything I’ve told you for two weeks. Per Tom Pelissero of NFL Network, Indianapolis was offered a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick for Jonathan Taylor, and they immediately rejected it.

Immediately.

Pelissero’s reporting is that the Colts “won’t deal him for anything other than a premium haul” — and the package they’re reportedly chasing is comparable to what Carolina got for Christian McCaffrey back in 2022.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Seahawks and Bears, tonight, 8:15 Eastern, Lumen Field, Monday Night Football on ABC and ESPN. Seattle is 4-3, defending Super Bowl champions. Chicago is 5-2. Seattle favored by three. Let’s do all of it — the trade that didn’t happen, the one that did, the Notre Dame story nobody’s told you, and the roster decision that has to be made in the next few hours.

First, a correction on the headline, and it matters.

Mike Macdonald didn’t pull off this move. John Schneider did.

And Macdonald said so himself, on the record: “Obviously we’re really excited to have the opportunity to acquire Quenton Nelson. I think John and his team did a great job of making that happen.”

He also explained why he stays out of it, and it’s one of my favorite quotes of the season: “Call me crazy. I don’t really like to react as things are kind of happening in the wild, because we like to be active in a lot of things... emotionally it’s just like, hey, let’s just stay the course, and we’ll coach the heck out of whoever ends up on our team.”

“We’ll coach the heck out of whoever ends up on our team.” That’s the division of labor. Schneider shops. Macdonald coaches. Give the general manager his credit.

Now the price comparison, because this is the whole story of Seattle’s deadline.

What Indianapolis turned down for a running back: a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick. Rejected on the spot.

What Seattle actually paid for Quenton Nelson: a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick.

Seattle got a six-time All-Pro offensive lineman for less than the Colts refused to accept for a running back.

And remember the mechanic that made it affordable: signing-bonus proration doesn’t travel in a trade, base salary does — and only the unpaid part. Nelson is on an expiring $16 million base, so Seattle pays a prorated $9.77 million for the rest of the year, and then he’s a free agent. No 2027 money. No 2028 money.

And the reason Seattle probably walked away from Taylor is the number I gave you last week: trading him before the deadline leaves Indianapolis eating about $17.6 million in dead money. That’s why the Colts are demanding a premium — they need the haul to justify the hit. And a premium for a 27-year-old running back who is averaging below his career yards-per-carry is exactly the kind of bill a smart front office refuses to pay.

Oh, and the man himself hasn’t asked to leave: Jonathan Taylor said “I haven’t heard anything, and I can’t control that,” and “I’m always proud to wear the horseshoe, proud to be here.”

And let me show you exactly how big “a McCaffrey-sized haul” actually is, because the number will end this debate.

In October 2022, San Francisco traded for Christian McCaffrey. They sent Carolina a 2023 second-round pick, a 2023 third-round pick, a 2023 fourth-round pick, and a 2024 fifth-round pick.

Four picks. A second, a third, a fourth and a fifth.

Now line the two situations up honestly, because they are not the same:

McCaffrey was 26 and under contract through 2025 — the buyer was getting three-plus seasons of a top-five running back. Jonathan Taylor is 27, signed through 2028 at $16 million in 2027 and $18 million in 2028, and is currently averaging below his career yards per carry with no hundred-yard game in eleven months.

So Indianapolis is asking for the McCaffrey package for an older back on a bigger contract having a worse season.

And they already proved they mean it: they turned down a 2027 second plus another pick on the spot.

Twelves, that’s four draft picks for ten games of a running back, and then thirty-four million dollars of decisions afterward. Seattle bought a six-time All-Pro offensive lineman for a third and a fifth, with no future money attached at all.

One of those is a bargain. The other is a bidding war. And Seattle is no longer in the bidding war.

So: Seattle made its deadline move eight days early, for a guard, at a discount. The deadline is November tenth, and I think the shopping is done.

Now the best story of the week, and almost nobody has told it. Quenton Nelson already has a teammate here — from nine years ago.

Julian Love played with Quenton Nelson at Notre Dame.

And twelves, listen to what Love said about meeting him:

“As an 18-year-old kid, Quenton was the first guy where I was like, ‘OK, that’s a pro.’”

“For a long time, I used to say he was the best player I ever played with. So it’s cool to have him on the team.”

“The best player I ever played with.” From a man who has spent years in the NFL and won a Super Bowl. And he’s talking about a guy he met in a college weight room.

And he painted the picture of Notre Dame’s 2017 season: his sophomore year, the left side of that line was Nelson at left guard and Mike McGlinchey at left tackle — McGlinchey went on to be a first-round NFL tackle — “and we had a really good running back. He was in the Heisman race, because we just ran to the left side.”

Read that again. A college running back entered the Heisman conversation because the offense simply ran left, behind Quenton Nelson and Mike McGlinchey, over and over.

That is the single best piece of scouting information available about the man playing left guard for Seattle tonight.

And Love’s character note is the part I actually loved most: “But every time I’d talk with him, it was always love — even though he was an older guy, even though he was an All-American already. I think that goes to how his character is built.”

An All-American upperclassman who was kind to an eighteen-year-old freshman. Nine years later they’re in the same locker room.

Love’s verdict on the trade: “Pretty excited. He’s a man’s man. He’s a walking Hall of Famer, which is pretty crazy.”

“A walking Hall of Famer.” That’s where that phrase came from — Julian Love, not a highlight channel.

And twelves, there’s a detail in that Notre Dame story worth sitting with, because it’s exactly what Seattle just bought.

A team ran the ball to one side of the field so relentlessly that the running back became a Heisman candidate. Not because of the back. Because of the two men blocking in front of him.

Now remember what Mike Macdonald told us about Nelson’s tape: “he’s a guy you want to run behind, I know that.” And: “he’s a great puller. I remember him pulling out in space and getting into the alleys. He’s a great athlete. Gets to the second level.”

And remember what play Seattle scored its final touchdown on against Kansas City: Macdonald told us it was a duo — a double-team run where two linemen move one defender backward together while the back reads the linebacker and picks a side.

So here is tonight’s simplest prediction: Seattle is going to run left, behind Quenton Nelson, more than they have all season. Against a Bears front ranked 26th in run defense DVOA that allows 4.4 yards a carry and has no starting running back available to answer with.

Nine years ago that formula put a college kid in the Heisman race. Tonight it just has to beat a 5-2 football team.

And the two men who have had to block him had things to say too.

Byron Murphy the Second, Seattle’s 306-pound defensive tackle: “His resume speaks for itself. He can do it all. He can maul you in the run game. He can pass protect.” And he admitted Nelson has given him problems in past matchups, and that having him now is “very helpful.”

When your own nose tackle says a guard gave him problems, that’s the review that counts.

Leonard Williams: “He’s a great player. Great decision to bring him over here. What I remember about Quenton is his effort, also his technique.”

And then Williams did something I want to highlight, because it’s the mark of a real veteran. He said he’s been advising Nelson on joining a new organization — and he’s been advising Anthony Bradford too.

Think about that. Leonard Williams is helping the man who just arrived and the man who just lost his starting job because of it. In the same week. Macdonald said there was “no way to sugarcoat” what happened to Bradford. Leonard Williams went and sugarcoated nothing — he just went and sat with him.

That’s the locker room Cooper Kupp was describing when he said “they really love one another.”

And Sam Darnold’s contribution, for the record: “Q coming in here, he adds a lot of experience. He’s a really talented football player.” Plus the line about the offensive line welcoming him “with open arms” and handling the situation “with grace.”

Now — the actual surprise move that has to happen in the next few hours.

Jadarian Price.

Seattle’s rookie first-round pick has missed four games on injured reserve with a chest injury. He has no game designation. He is healthy. He was limited Thursday and a full participant the last two days.

But he’s still on injured reserve. And to play him tonight, Seattle has to activate him — which requires a spot on the 53-man roster.

And Seattle doesn’t have a spare one, because they used it on Quenton Nelson and waived Christian Haynes, who Miami claimed within a day.

So when Macdonald was asked whether Price plays, he said: “If we were to make a move, I think that would happen on Monday, to my knowledge. We’ll see if that’s what we do. We got some things going on roster-wise that we’re working through right now.”

And when a reporter pressed — he has no injury designation, so if he’s not activated it isn’t medical, it’s something else? — Macdonald said:

“We’re just going to let the transaction speak for itself on that one.”

Twelves, that’s today. That decision lands this afternoon. Either Jadarian Price gets activated and some other Seahawk loses his place on the roster, or the rookie first-rounder watches a prime-time home game in street clothes while completely healthy.

“Let the transaction speak for itself” means there’s a name coming that nobody has said out loud yet. Watch the wire this afternoon.

And the man who IS definitely playing: Zach Charbonnet. Here’s the full timeline, because it deserves to be said properly.

He tore his left ACL in the divisional round in January. Surgery in February. Seattle activated him from the PUP list on October twenty-second. He was inactive last Sunday against the Chiefs. He was a full participant Thursday and Saturday, limited Friday. And tonight he makes his 2026 debut.

Macdonald: “We have a plan for Zach.” And his tribute: “To come back on the timetable he is... is remarkable. Zach’s a guy that kind of works in the shadows. Hopefully people understand what kind of stud this guy is.”

Expect a managed snap count. Macdonald told us the plan “might waver a little bit throughout the game, but that’s okay.” Don’t expect twenty carries. Do expect him on third down, because his own head coach called him “as good as they get blocking out of the backfield” — and Chicago is fourth in the NFL in pressure rate against a Seattle line with a backup left tackle.

And one opportunity nobody’s talking about: Tory Horton.

Rashid Shaheed is out with a knee injury. That’s Seattle’s deep threat and punt returner. Macdonald said “he’s doing well” and “we’ll recalibrate next week” — so this is week-to-week, not month-to-month.

But tonight somebody has to fill those snaps, and Tory Horton is the obvious candidate. A 2025 fifth-round pick out of Colorado State who has flashed in camp as a big-play receiver.

I’m not going to oversell him. He’s a fifth-rounder in his second year with limited production. But this is how careers start — somebody gets hurt, somebody else gets fifteen snaps in prime time, and either he does something with them or he doesn’t. Watch him tonight.

Now the schedule argument, because somebody made a good one and I want to give them credit and then make it honest.

A Lions fan channel laid this out, and the math is real: win tonight and the next two games are Arizona and Las Vegas. Both are short West Coast trips — no flying east, no body-clock games, no one-o’clock Eastern kickoffs. Win those and Seattle is 7-3 after Week 10.

And he’s right that 7-3 would put Seattle near the top of the NFC, because Detroit and Minnesota play each other this week, so one of them takes a loss. Minnesota is the last undefeated team in football.

But let me be the honest one here: nothing on that schedule is free, and the proof is in Seattle’s own loss column.

This team already lost to Washington. Seattle has lost three games by a combined eight points. The 2026 Seahawks do not win games because the opponent’s record is bad; they win them by running forty-one times and holding the ball for thirty-six minutes. Arizona and Las Vegas are winnable. Winnable is not won.

And I’ll say this for the Lions fans who made the video: they called a Lions-Seahawks NFC Championship game. I’d take that matchup. Detroit is a real football team and that would be a hell of a January. Respect for covering a team you don’t root for, honestly and without agenda.

Now the title, and here’s my verdict.

“Mike Macdonald just pulled off a surprise move” is half right. The move is real and it’s a surprise — but it was John Schneider’s, and it happened six days ago.

The actual surprise today is the one you can still watch unfold: whether Seattle clears a 53-man spot to activate a healthy first-round rookie, and the fact that Seattle has gone quiet on Jonathan Taylor while four other teams keep calling Indianapolis.

Sometimes the surprise move is the restraint.

My pick, one more time: Seahawks 24, Bears 20.

How it happens: Chicago is 26th in run defense DVOA and allows 4.4 yards a carry, and tonight they play without their starting running back, against a defense that is first in the NFL in opponent yards per play, allows the fewest explosive passes in football, and hasn’t given up a hundred-yard rusher in thirty-six straight games.

Seattle does what Sam Darnold described: first down, second down, first down. They run duo behind Quenton Nelson, they hold the ball past thirty-four minutes, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba passes Lance Alworth on his first catch of the night — he needs four yards to move into second place for most receiving yards through seven games in the Super Bowl era.

The thing that beats us is turnovers. Chicago is fourth in pressure rate with ten takeaways, Josh Jones is at left tackle, and four of Darnold’s eight interceptions have come with a defender in his face. Protect the quarterback and this is a comfortable night. Don’t, and it’s a shootout we’re not built for.

Let me close.

Tonight, a man who tore his ACL in the playoffs runs out of that tunnel for the first time this season. A six-time All-Pro plays his first game in Seahawks green, with a college teammate from 2017 watching from the other side of the ball. A backup left tackle protects the blind side of a quarterback nobody wanted two weeks ago. And somewhere this afternoon, a transaction speaks for itself.
