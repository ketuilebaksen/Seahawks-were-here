NOW — the run scheme answer, and twelves, this explains Seattle's whole season on the ground. I covered it yesterday but it's too important to skip.

A reporter asked why Seattle majors in outside zone when a lot of the league has moved toward duo.

"A lot of the league right now is — we call it MID ZONE. But you're right, WE MAJOR IN WIDE ZONE. THAT'S WHERE WE DECIDED TO HANG OUR HAT."

And the comparison: "WIDE ZONE IS A LITTLE BIT MORE OF A VOLATILE PLAY. There's A LITTLE MORE VARIANCE. DUO — it's more of a HAT ON A HAT. Tends to create LESS EXPLOSIVES on average."

Twelves, here's the translation. Wide zone is the horizontal run — the whole line moves sideways, the back races to the edge looking for a crease. It's HIGH VARIANCE: when it hits, it's twenty yards. When it doesn't, you lose two, because everybody ran sideways.

Duo is vertical — two men double-team one defender and drive him backward while the back reads a linebacker. LOW VARIANCE. Reliable four yards. Rarely thirty.

AND THAT IS THE ENTIRE EXPLANATION FOR WHY THIS RUN GAME FEELS MADDENING. Two yards. One yard. Minus one. Eighteen. Three. Twenty-four. That's not broken. That's the SHAPE of the play.

And then the admission almost no coach makes: "YOU COULD ARGUE WE'RE PROBABLY MORE EFFECTIVE RIGHT NOW AT SOME OF THE INSIDE STUFF THAN SOME OF THE OUTSIDE STUFF. THAT DOESN'T MEAN THAT WE'RE GOING TO GET AWAY FROM IT."

His own identity is wide zone, and he just said they're currently better at the other thing — and they're not changing. Because wide zone isn't a play, it's a SKILL SET. Years of footwork and timing. You can't swap it out in Week 12 and you can't get it back.
