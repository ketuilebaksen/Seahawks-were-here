I’m going to be honest with you in the first ten seconds, because that’s what this channel is for.

Seattle did not add a weapon on Friday. Seattle ruled out seven players.

Sam Darnold. Charles Cross. Quenton Nelson. Uchenna Nwosu. Rashid Shaheed. Leonard Williams. Brandon Pili. All out. Connor O’Toole questionable.

That’s the Friday press conference. It’s a subtraction, not an addition, and I won’t pretend otherwise.

But stay with me — because there are two real additions buried in this, and one of them is a spot on the fifty-three man roster that just opened up with the deadline forty-eight hours away.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Seattle is four and four, Arizona is at Lumen Field on Sunday, and Mike Macdonald just delivered the hardest injury report of this season. Let’s go through all of it — and then the prediction I promised you.

THE BIG ONE: Quenton Nelson needs a knee procedure.

Here are Macdonald’s exact words: “Quenton’s going to be out. He has a — we’re going to do a minor procedure. He’s week to week at this point. We’re not going to put him on IR. There’s a timeline right now that warrants not IR.”

A minor knee procedure. Week to week. Nine days after Seattle traded a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick to get him.

And here’s the detail that landed hardest: Quenton Nelson played every single offensive snap on Monday night. On that knee.

Now the question that had to be asked, and credit to the reporter who asked it: did Seattle know about the knee when they made the deal?

Macdonald’s answer, word for word: “I can’t give you like all the details on what it was and what we — you know, obviously passed the physical. So I think two things are true. One, that it was something he was dealing with coming out of whatever game he was in before the trade. And I think it’s been aggravated since he got here.”

And earlier: “He had something he was dealing with when he got here, and we reimaged it, and it was aggravated to some degree. So we’ve kind of done the due diligence and everybody agrees it’s best to take care of it now and play the long game.”

Let me translate that carefully, because I won’t turn it into a scandal and I won’t pretend it’s nothing.

He passed the physical — stated plainly, and it matters. Something pre-existing came with him from Indianapolis. Seattle re-imaged it here. It got worse here. And the medical consensus is: fix it now and get the whole second half of the season out of him, instead of taping it up and risking the rest.

That’s the right decision and an uncomfortable one. You don’t trade premium draft capital for a guard and watch him have a procedure in his second week. But “play the long game” is correct, and the man played every snap of a Monday night game before anybody fixed anything. Nobody here is the villain.

And the “no IR” part is the key, so let me do the math nobody else will do for you.

Injured reserve means a minimum of four games. Gone. No negotiation. Macdonald said the timeline doesn’t warrant it — so Seattle believes this is shorter than four games.

Now look at the calendar. Week 9 is Arizona. Week 10 is at Las Vegas on November fifteenth. Week 11 is Seattle’s bye. Week 12 is at San Francisco on November twenty-ninth.

From Sunday to that 49ers game is three full weeks of calendar — with only two football games inside it.

And listen to how Macdonald framed the line decision: “not having a specific timetable, and having it be, you know, at least this game and X amount of practices, and then going into the bye — let’s put those guys back in where they banked all those reps.”

“And then going into the bye.” He just told you the plan. The bye is doing the heavy lifting on Nelson’s recovery, the realistic target is San Francisco on November twenty-ninth, and the schedule is handing Seattle the time to get there.

UPDATE TWO: the offensive line goes back — the addition I’ll actually defend.

Macdonald: “AB will go back to right guard, and Grey will go back to left guard.”

Anthony Bradford to right guard. Grey Zabel to left guard. Both back where they’ve played their entire careers.

Twelves, I argued for this on this channel. When Seattle slid Zabel to the right side I said the risk was continuity — asking a young lineman to learn a new side mid-season. And I spent a whole video arguing against trading Anthony Bradford, because he was the only guard on the roster with forty career starts at right guard. “You do not sell your insurance policy in the same week you create the risk.”

Ten days later, the insurance policy is the starter.

And Macdonald’s reasoning is exactly that: “let’s put those guys back in where they banked all those reps. I think that was the most wise decision.”

“Where they banked all those reps.” Reps are currency, and both of these men already have money in the bank at those spots.

And here’s how fresh it is: asked when the decision happened — “the news on Quenton is fairly recent, so that decision happened recently. Yesterday. Within the last twenty-four hours.”

Seattle rebuilt its offensive line interior inside of twenty-four hours, forty-eight hours before kickoff.

UPDATE THREE: and this is my favorite moment of the entire press conference.

A reporter asked him to walk through the guard situation, and Mike Macdonald said this, completely deadpan:

“Yeah, so actually the grand plan was to really just try to shake everything up, because we felt like we were too comfortable.”

And then he snorted. Because he was joking.

That’s a head coach making fun of his own season, out loud, at a podium, two days before a division game.

And here’s why that’s a good sign, not a bad one. A coach who is drowning does not make jokes. A coach who has lost the room does not dryly suggest to fifteen reporters that all this lineup chaos was the plan. That’s a man who is annoyed, not panicked. And after Monday night, annoyed is exactly what I want him to be.

UPDATE FOUR: Anthony Bradford — and this one is genuinely moving, so I want to read you all of it.

A reporter pointed out that Macdonald had said last week this might be hard on Bradford, and now he’s one play away. Macdonald’s answer:

“That’s a great point. Anthony, I mean, he really deserves a tip of the cap just on how he’s handled the whole process. Very impressive. I mean, as difficult as that situation is for him specifically, how he’s handled it has been spectacular.”

And then the part that tells you what this staff says behind closed doors:

“And part of the messaging too is like — look, at some point, this isn’t like you’re not playing football for us anymore. So you’ve got to keep approaching the process the same way. It’s happened earlier than we expected, obviously, but this is the way things go.”

And: “I’m really confident in his ability to go play great football for us, and I know he’s excited for the opportunity.”

Twelves, stack that timeline up. Ten days ago Anthony Bradford lost his starting job to a trade. Last week people were arguing Seattle should ship him out for a late-round pick. He kept showing up. On Sunday he starts at right guard in a division game.

And this is his contract year — a man whose entire financial future runs through the next nine games, who handled losing his job like a professional for a week and a half.

Tip of the cap is right.

UPDATE FIVE: the full casualty list, and one name on it genuinely stopped me.

Ruled out: Sam Darnold with the quad and the ribs. Charles Cross with the ankle. Quenton Nelson with the knee. Uchenna Nwosu with the ankle. Rashid Shaheed with the knee. Leonard Williams with the stinger. Brandon Pili with the back.

Questionable: Connor O’Toole with the foot.

Now here’s the one. Leonard Williams is about to miss his first game since Week 4 of the 2024 season.

Sit with that. Through two full seasons and a playoff run, Leonard Williams has not missed a football game. He’s the most quietly reliable player on this roster.

And on Sunday, a stinger ends it. That’s the most underrated thing about this defense, and most people won’t even register that it’s gone.

UPDATE SIX: Macdonald’s honesty about why none of them got better.

Asked whether anything changed with Leonard Williams, who had sounded like he had a chance earlier in the week: “All these guys just — nothing, you know. It’s just the way it goes. They just didn’t improve.”

No spin. No medical word salad. “They just didn’t improve.”

And then the question that actually matters: on a normal week, would some of these guys have played? Macdonald: “That might be — it might be a factor. Might be a factor.”

He said it twice. Read that plainly: a Monday night game cost Seattle two days of recovery, and the head coach just told you it may have cost him players on Sunday. That’s the hidden price of prime time nobody puts in the highlight package.

And on Charles Cross: “you just got to get over those hurdles so you can get out there in practice. He’s working his tail off. All these guys are working hard. Just haven’t been able to make it to the game.”

“Haven’t been able to make it to the game.” The hurdle isn’t the injury any more — it’s practice. You have to clear practice to clear Sunday, and Cross hasn’t cleared practice in two weeks.

UPDATE SEVEN: and here’s the good news in the middle of all this.

Asked whether there’s new IR talk for Leonard Williams or Rashid Shaheed: “Not to my knowledge. No. No.”

Nobody is going on injured reserve. Not Nelson, not Leonard Williams, not Shaheed. This team believes every one of them is back inside four games — and one of those four weeks is a bye.

UPDATE EIGHT: the quarterback, and two words that are the best news in this press conference.

“Drew’s going to start. Yep.”

And on the week: “He’s done a great job, and Jalen Milroe has done a great job as well. So it’s been a tag team effort to get ready. I thought both guys — loved their attitude. The details, all those things, they’re in a good spot.”

“A tag team effort.” Two quarterbacks splitting a game week.

And then the part nobody will headline. Asked how much he’s had to govern Lock’s throwing: “We’ve had to manage it. More precautionary than anything else.”

“More precautionary than anything else.”

Compare that to Wednesday, when the line was “he’s able to throw — how much, we’re working through that right now” and “he’s not going to take 8,000 throws.” That sounded like a real limitation. Friday’s version is “precautionary.”

That’s a downgrade in concern — and Drew Lock has no game-status designation at all. Not questionable. Nothing. For a quarterback inactive the last two games with an elbow problem, a clean designation is a genuinely good outcome.

UPDATE NINE: Jalen Milroe, and a real nugget about his development.

Asked about his growth from third to second on the depth chart: “He hasn’t taken a lot of reps at the one, so to see him do that and operate the offense has been impressive. He’s been working really hard, and he’ll be ready to go if we need him.”

“He hasn’t taken a lot of reps at the one.” That’s the honest state of Jalen Milroe — a backup who until this week had barely run the first-team offense in practice. And the coach’s read on his first real week of it is “impressive.”

He has still never thrown a pass in an NFL game. He is now one elbow away from doing it, in a division game, at home.

UPDATE TEN: Sam Darnold’s week — the detail I’ll remember.

Asked what Darnold has been able to do: “He’s just working in rehab and going through that process. So he’s kind of like a coach on the field right now. I’ve probably talked to him the most during practice I’ve ever had, because normally he’s taking all the plays. So it’s been fun.”

“I’ve probably talked to him the most during practice I’ve ever had.”

A quarterback with a cracked rib and a bad quad spent his week on the practice field coaching, and his head coach called it fun.

That’s two press conferences in a row where Macdonald volunteered something flattering about Sam Darnold unprompted. Last week it was the rib and “he’s tough as nails.” This week it’s that the man can’t throw, so he’s teaching instead. I’ve told you for two weeks that nobody in that building has wavered on him. This is the third piece of evidence.

UPDATE ELEVEN: Charbonnet and Price — Macdonald politely declined.

Asked whether the snap constraints are still on Jadarian Price and Zach Charbonnet: “There are some parameters that we’re working with, for who it is and to what extent. Probably not going to tell everybody on that one.”

He said no — politely, but no. And good. Why publish your running back usage plan to the team you play in forty-eight hours?

But he gave something real: “We’ve had some moving pieces on offense for X amount of weeks now. But as it starts to calm down, and guys get more reps and more chemistry, I think that’ll pay dividends for us.”

“As it starts to calm down.” A head coach telling you the chaos has a shelf life.

UPDATE TWELVE: on Arizona with Gardner Minshew the Second — a smart, honest answer.

Asked how Arizona looks different with Minshew at quarterback: “I’m not really sure yet.”

He just said it. He doesn’t know. And then the real football reason: “you can go off the preseason stuff... I mean, they were in comeback mode last week, so it’s hard to tell how they’re going to operate on early downs.”

That’s an important point, so let me break it down. Minshew’s seventeen-of-twenty-three, 188-yard, three-touchdown half against Dallas happened in comeback football — and comeback football is a different sport. Down fourteen in the second half, the defense plays soft, keeps everything in front of it and trades yards for clock. The completions get handed to you underneath.

So what did Seattle learn about Minshew from that tape? Almost nothing about first and ten. What he does on early downs against honest coverage — that film doesn’t exist from last week.

His approach: “the best you can do is go off the tape you’ve seen and the way you feel like you’ve been attacked by other teams, and try to project that” — plus “being able to adjust throughout the game.”

“Adjust throughout the game.” If you watched the Chiefs game, you know this staff can. If you watched the Bears game, you know it didn’t happen.
