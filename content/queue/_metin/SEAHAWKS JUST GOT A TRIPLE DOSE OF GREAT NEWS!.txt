Sam Darnold is out on Sunday.

And the man replacing him beat this exact opponent 31-7 earlier this season, with three touchdowns, all three of them to Jaxon Smith-Njigba.

Seahawks Nation, that’s not spin. That’s the actual situation. Mike Macdonald has ruled Sam Darnold out of Sunday’s game against the Arizona Cardinals with a quad injury. Drew Lock is starting. And Drew Lock’s only start of the 2026 season was a thirty-one to seven win over the Arizona Cardinals.

This is James, welcome back to Seahawks Were Here. Seattle is 4-4, on a six-day week, coming off a 34-17 loss, with the trade deadline on November tenth. And twelves, there genuinely is a triple dose of good news in this week’s injury report — but I’m not burying the headline to get to it. Let’s go.

First: everything we know about Sam Darnold.

He’s out Sunday with a quad injury. The official report lists it as “quadriceps, ribs.” And we know where part of that came from: he hurt his ribs early in the Bears game — the one he finished, seventeen of thirty-four for 188 yards with two touchdowns.

He played a full game on hurt ribs and nobody knew.

Macdonald’s framing: Darnold is “considered week to week,” and he is NOT going on injured reserve.

That second part matters enormously. Remember the rule: injured reserve is a minimum of four games. Seattle isn’t doing it. Which means they believe he’s back before four games are up — exactly the same signal they gave us with Charles Cross last week.

Week to week. Not a month. That’s the read.

And here’s the strange symmetry: Sam Darnold also missed Seattle’s Week 2 game against these same Arizona Cardinals — that time with a glute injury. He is going to finish this season having missed both Arizona games and no others.

Now Drew Lock, and this is good news dose number one.

Lock’s numbers this season: seventy-three percent completion. Eight point eight yards per attempt. Four touchdowns, zero interceptions. A 127.3 passer rating.

Four touchdowns and no interceptions. For context, Sam Darnold has eight touchdowns and nine interceptions.

And the start itself: Week 2, at Arizona. Drew Lock went nineteen of twenty-six for 235 yards and three touchdowns. Seattle won 31-7.

All three touchdowns went to Jaxon Smith-Njigba.

Twelves, think about that for Sunday. The quarterback has already played this opponent once this year and put up three touchdowns and a twenty-four-point win. He’s seen their coverages. He’s thrown against their corners. He has the tape and he has the result.

Macdonald said this about him before that first start: “Drew’s a proven really good player in this league, and when he’s started, he’s played really good football.”

Now the caveats, because I’m not going to sell you a fantasy.

One: Drew Lock’s own elbow is a problem. He was limited in practice Wednesday with a right elbow injury, and Macdonald said: “He’s able to throw. How much, we’re working through that right now.”

“How much, we’re working through that.” That is not a coach describing a healthy arm. This elbow is why Lock was inactive against Kansas City and served as the emergency third quarterback instead. Watch Friday’s report closely.

And if Lock can’t go, the backup is Jalen Milroe.

Two: Drew Lock’s career as a fill-in starter is genuinely uneven. One analysis counted five starts in which he replaced the previous week’s starter, and his teams went 2-3 in them. The honest summary was that his fill-in work has been “all over the map” apart from one blowout win — and that blowout was the Arizona game.

So the accurate version is: Drew Lock has one great game against this specific team, strong efficiency numbers in limited work, a sore elbow, and a career that says don’t assume. That’s the truth, and it’s still better news than most of you expected when you heard Darnold was out.

Good news dose number two, and this one is quietly the best thing in the report.

Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price do not appear on Wednesday’s injury report at all.

Not out. Not limited. Not listed.

Twelves, do you understand how long we’ve waited for that? Charbonnet tore his ACL in the divisional round and had surgery February twentieth. Price, the rookie first-round pick, spent four games on injured reserve with a chest injury. For two months, this channel has been covering a running back room that was Emanuel Wilson and a prayer.

As of Wednesday, Seattle has Charbonnet, Price, Emanuel Wilson and George Holani — and none of them have an injury designation.

And here’s why it matters more than it sounds. On Monday night, Charbonnet’s carefully managed return got spent by the scoreboard. Macdonald told us: “you can’t ask guys to come back and play a certain amount of snaps. Obviously the game shook out a certain way and that affected how we played our guys.”

He waited eight months and the game script took his snaps.

On Sunday, with a healthy room and a quarterback who needs the run game to carry him, that doesn’t happen. A 4-4 team starting a backup quarterback is going to run the football. For the first time all year, Seattle can.

Good news dose number three: three full participants and one real recovery.

Nick Emmanwori — full participation, with a shoulder injury on the report.

Full. After playing every single defensive snap against Kansas City, after the hip tightness from three position rooms, after a shoulder showed up this week. He practiced in full on a short week. That man is made of something unusual.

Tory Horton — full participation, with a hand injury.

And Horton matters right now. Rashid Shaheed did not practice Wednesday, which means Horton is in line for real snaps again — and he’s the man who caught Darnold’s two-point conversion on Monday night. I told you to watch him before the game; he made the scoring summary. Now he’s healthy and the door is still open.

Tyrice Knight — full participation, with a thigh injury. The linebacker Aden Durde described as the definition of a Seahawks player: “when your name’s called, you’ve got to be prepared, ready, and ready to execute.”

And Uchenna Nwosu was limited. Last week he was doubtful and didn’t play. Doubtful to limited in seven days is real progress for an edge rusher on an ankle.

Now the honest other side, because a good-news video that hides the bad news is a lie.

Did not practice Wednesday: Charles Cross (ankle). Rashid Shaheed (knee). Leonard Williams (stinger). Connor O’Toole (foot). Brandon Pili (back).

That’s Seattle’s starting left tackle, its deep threat, and one of the best defensive tackles in football — all on the sideline on day one of a six-day week.

But here’s what Macdonald said about them: Leonard Williams, Charles Cross and Rashid Shaheed all “have a chance to play,” and nobody has been ruled out. His words: “Nothing definitive this early in the week.”

On a six-day week, a Wednesday DNP is not the same as a Wednesday DNP on a normal week. Macdonald told us the plan himself: “this isn’t time for studying. It’s just — let’s attack the tape, let’s make sure we’re all on the same page.” Veterans rest on short weeks by design. Friday is the report that matters.

Also limited: Anthony Bradford (knee), Quenton Nelson (knee), Velus Jones Jr. (hamstring) — and Jaxon Smith-Njigba was limited for rest, not injury. Quenton Nelson on a knee, in his second week, is worth watching. And with Nelson limited, Anthony Bradford is suddenly relevant again — exactly the insurance argument I made for keeping him.

And now the thing that makes Sunday genuinely weird: Arizona is in the same boat.

The Cardinals’ quarterback on Sunday is Gardner Minshew the Second — because Jacoby Brissett did not practice Wednesday.

It is a backup-versus-backup game.

And Arizona’s report is long: Budda Baker, Josh Sweat, Isaac Seumalo and Hjalte Froholdt all did not practice (listed as rest, not injury). Jayden Williams (knee), Bam Knight (concussion) and Jon Gaines the Second (ankle) did not practice. Walter Nolen the Third (ankle), Isaiah Adams (ankle) and Michael Wilson (ribs) were limited.

Two banged-up teams, two backup quarterbacks, at Lumen Field, in November. Whoever runs the ball better wins. That’s the whole game.

Now Cooper Kupp — because he gave the sober version of Monday night, and it’s the counterweight to everything I just told you.

On the offense: “Not good enough. Way too many three-and-outs, way too many short drives, and just putting our defense back out on the field. Just not holding up our end, I guess. We’ve got to be better than that and find some solutions.”

“Not holding up our end.”

Twelves, that’s the offense accepting responsibility for the defense giving up 34 points. Seattle’s defense allowed its most points of the season, and the receiver’s explanation is that his own unit kept handing the ball back. One for eleven on third down. 252 total yards. He’s right.

And he gave Chicago credit, same as Macdonald did four separate times: “Kudos to the Bears. That was a very good defense. But we’ve got to be better. We have to be able to execute better and at the end of the day keep the ball moving forward and make the plays that are there for us.”

“Keep the ball moving forward.” That’s Darnold’s exact phrase from Friday. Same building, same sentence.

And then Kupp gave the single best piece of football teaching of the week.

Asked about Darnold having to improvise and extend plays:

“I think he’s been tremendous off schedule over the last couple of years... he’s a pretty dynamic quarterback when he does get out of the pocket. And I think there’s a lot of things that go into that in terms of us distributing the field and understanding that the play continues — the play, really the second play starts once he gets out of the pocket. And we’ve got to do a better job of being able to get open and find ways to let him get the ball out of his hands when he does extend like that.”

“The second play starts once he gets out of the pocket.”

That is the clearest description of scramble drill I’ve ever heard from a player. When the quarterback leaves the pocket, every route you ran is void. The original play is dead and a brand-new one begins — and in that new play, the receiver’s job is to break off his route, find grass, and get into the quarterback’s vision.

And Cooper Kupp put the failure on the receivers. Not on the protection. Not on the quarterback. “We’ve got to do a better job of being able to get open and find ways to let him get the ball out of his hands.”

A quarterback was sacked three times and the Hall-of-Fame-caliber receiver in the room says the men downfield have to be better. That’s accountability.

And then he broke down Chicago’s coverage like a coordinator.

“They had a good plan. There’s a mix of some of the stuff that we’ve seen, but also playing a mixture of some four-man stuff, a few different two-man looks, doing a good job with the safeties. They played well off each other in terms of understanding the times they can be aggressive at safety and be able to cut things and play low based on the pressure they were generating — or expecting to generate — and also being able to take away stuff down the field when they needed to.”

Let me translate that, because it’s precise.

“Four-man stuff” means rushing four and dropping seven. “Two-man looks” means Two-Man Under — man coverage underneath with two deep safeties over the top. It’s the coverage you call when you want to take away everything deep and dare the quarterback to hold the ball.

And “cut things and play low based on the pressure” means the safeties were reading the rush. If the pressure was getting home, they jumped the shorter routes. If it wasn’t, they stayed deep.

That’s a defense that let its front four dictate its back end. And it explains the whole night: three sacks, a thirty-one percent pressure rate, one for eleven on third down, and no explosive plays except one 43-yard touchdown.

And here’s Kupp’s bottom line, which no coach would say this bluntly: “We’ve got to do a better job adjusting some of those ways. And at the end of the day, we’ve got to make plays as players and make these plays happen.”

Now the number everybody is arguing about, and it’s astonishing.

A reporter told Kupp that Jaxon Smith-Njigba leads the NFL in target share. And twelves, I went and found the figure:

Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s target share is 45.8 percent, per SumerSports — the highest in football. Second place is Justin Jefferson at 39.1 percent.

Nearly forty-six percent of Seattle’s throws go to one man.

Kupp’s answer was gracious and correct: “Jaxon is a great player. He’s going to command targets. He’s one of, if not the best receiver in the league right now, and so that’s just going to be part of the deal. We all have a part of it. If he’s open, the ball’s going his way. We’ve got to find ways to continue to compete — and you’ve got to compete without the ball as well. And when the ball does come your way, you’ve got to make a play.”

“You’ve got to compete without the ball as well.”

That is Cooper Kupp — a triple crown winner with eighteen catches this season — saying that blocking, selling routes and occupying defenders is part of his job description now.

And it lines up exactly with what Macdonald said Monday night: “to me, it’s inputs... if we’re dialed in on what we should be doing, we’re way more efficient, and then the ball is distributed more evenly.”

Neither of them is asking for more targets. Both of them are saying the concentration is a symptom of being behind schedule.

And here’s the delicious footnote for Sunday: the last time Drew Lock started, all three of his touchdowns went to Jaxon Smith-Njigba.

The target share may be about to get even more concentrated. And if it produces 31-7 again, nobody in Seattle is going to complain.

Now the title, and here’s my verdict.

“Triple dose of great news” is real — I found three. One: the replacement quarterback has a 31-7 win over this exact opponent, a 127.3 passer rating and four touchdowns with no interceptions. Two: Charbonnet and Price are completely off the injury report, so the backfield is finally whole. Three: Emmanwori, Horton and Knight are full participants and Nwosu went from doubtful to limited.

But the transcript this video came from isn’t good news at all — it’s Cooper Kupp soberly explaining three-and-outs and Two-Man coverage. And the actual headline of the week is that Sam Darnold is out.

So if you clicked expecting pure sunshine: the news is genuinely mixed, and the biggest item is a starting quarterback in street clothes. I’m not going to pretend otherwise. But I’d rather be 4-4 with Drew Lock against Arizona than 4-4 with a quarterback on a bad quad and bruised ribs.
